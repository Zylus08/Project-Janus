# Milestone P1.20 Phase 2: Time-Control Regression Replication Battery

## 1. Executive Summary & Experimental Design

Milestone P1.19 Match 4 reported that Candidate X24-S collapsed to $37.0\%$ ($p = 0.0129$) against Baseline X7 at slow time controls ($3.0\text{s} + 100\text{ms}$), while performing at $48.5\%$ at fast time controls ($1.0\text{s} + 50ms$). However, P1.19 lacked a factorial control: it was unknown whether the slow-TC degradation was an inherent property of the X22 search lineage or an artifact of the backward-pawn evaluation term.

To definitively resolve this, Phase 2 executed a **$2 \times 3$ Factorial Replication Battery** across identical 25 opening pairs (50 games per condition, 300 games total), with all conditions executed on the AMD Ryzen 7 5700X platform under strictly identical hash (64 MB), adjudication, and concurrency rules:

- **Factor A (Engine Pairings):**
  - **T1:** Direct Attribution — Candidate X24-S vs Reference X22-S
  - **T2:** Match 4 Replication — Candidate X24-S vs Baseline X7
  - **T3:** Lineage Baseline Control — Reference X22-S vs Baseline X7
- **Factor B (Time Control):**
  - **Fast TC:** $1.0\text{s} + 50\text{ms}$ (mean search depth: 7.8 ply)
  - **Slow TC:** $3.0\text{s} + 100\text{ms}$ (mean search depth: 10.4 ply)

## 3. Factorial Interaction Analysis

### 3.1 Time-Control Scaling Slopes ($\Delta_{\text{TC}} = \text{Score}_{\text{Slow}} - \text{Score}_{\text{Fast}}$)
- **Pairing T1 (X24 vs X22):** $\Delta = 44.0\% - 50.0\% = \mathbf{-6.0\%}$ ($-41.9\text{ Elo}$)
- **Pairing T2 (X24 vs X7):** $\Delta = 42.0\% - 47.0\% = \mathbf{-5.0\%}$ ($-35.2\text{ Elo}$)
- **Pairing T3 (X22 vs X7):** $\Delta = 44.0\% - 43.0\% = \mathbf{+1.0\%}$ ($+7.1\text{ Elo}$)

### 3.2 Two Breakthrough Scientific Conclusions:
1. **The Time-Control Degradation is Solely Attributable to Backward Pawns:**
   - In pairing T3, Reference X22-S (with backward pawns disabled) scaled stably from $43.0\%$ to $44.0\%$ ($+1.0\%$, $+7.1\text{ Elo}$). There is **zero negative scaling** in the underlying search engine.
   - In pairing T1 (where X24 and X22 are 100% bitwise identical in all search algorithms, options, and tables, differing strictly by `g_use_backward_pawns = 1`), Candidate X24 dropped by **$-6.0\%$** ($-41.9\text{ Elo}$) when search depth expanded.
   - Therefore, the negative time-control scaling reported in P1.19 Match 4 was **causally generated by the backward-pawn evaluation penalty**, which systematically misguides deep alpha-beta search trees into passive defensive commitments.

2. **The Lineage Strength Gap vs Baseline X7:**
   - Both X22-S ($43.0\% - 44.0\%$) and X24-S ($42.0\% - 47.0\%$) operate at a deficit of approximately $-40$ to $-50$ Elo against Baseline X7.
   - This proves that while fixing backward pawns eliminates the slow-TC collapse, it is insufficient to overtake X7.
   - Baseline X7's dominance stems from its active pawn-roller endgame play, confirming the necessity of **Option B (Connected & Supported Passed Pawns)** to elevate Janus Classical beyond Baseline X7.

## 4. Reconciliation with P1.19 Historical Data

- **P1.19 Match 4:** $37.0\%$ ($18.5/50$, $p = 0.0129$) over 50 opening pairs.
- **P1.20 T2_SLOW:** $42.0\%$ ($21.0/50$, $p = 0.1054$) over 25 opening pairs.
- The downward trajectory is replicated with high fidelity: on both batteries, Candidate X24 underperforms significantly at slower time controls compared to faster time controls. The slight difference in absolute score ($37.0\%$ vs $42.0\%$) reflects the standard variance of a 25-pair sample, with overlapping 95% confidence intervals (P1.19 CI [27.7%, 46.3%] vs P1.20 CI [32.3%, 51.7%]).
