### 3. Tournament Opening Contamination

The audit crossed the 40,736 training positions with the 100 opening positions from `independent_openings_v056.json`:
- **Openings present in Training Set:** **48 positions**.
- **Root Cause:** The Phase 1 tournament games used to construct the dataset (e.g. `match1_r1_vs_x22s_fast.pgn`, `match3_r1_vs_x7_fast.pgn`) were played from the `independent_openings_v056.json` opening book. Because the extraction filter accepted positions from ply 8 onward, early middlegame positions immediately following the opening book transpositions entered the training corpus.
- **Qualification Requirement:** Model H1 cannot be claimed to be an *opening-blind* evaluator when tested on `independent_openings_v056.json`. However, because H1 scored 0.0% against X7, X22-S, and Stockfish, this contamination did not artificially inflate its competitive results.

### 4. Statistical Distributions & Systematic Dataset Biases

The audit uncovered four major statistical properties of the training corpus:

#### 4.1 Side-to-Move Balance
- White to move: $20,454$ ($50.2\%$)
- Black to move: $20,282$ ($49.8\%$)
- Perfect symmetry across sides to move.

#### 4.2 Target Distribution: High Draw Prevalence
- Target $1.0$ (Side to Move Won): $10,262$ ($25.2\%$)
- Target $0.0$ (Side to Move Lost): $10,309$ ($25.3\%$)
- Target $0.5$ (Game Drawn): $20,165$ ($49.5\%$)
- **Impact:** Nearly half of all positions have a target of $0.5$. In binary cross-entropy optimization, this acts as a heavy regularizing drag toward the origin (logit $0.0$). This directly contributes to the severe centipawn score compression observed in Phase 2.

#### 4.3 Phase & Material Distribution
- Mean ply depth: $43.8$ plies.
- In-check positions: $8.1\%$.
- Piece count distribution: Centers between 18 and 28 pieces (middlegames), with fewer than $5\%$ endgame positions ($\le 6$ pieces).
- **Impact:** The network has virtually zero training signal on theoretical endgames (opposition, king and pawn endings, rook endings).

#### 4.4 Source Engine Quality Bias (Self-Play Noise)
All 582 source games were generated by historical Janus Phase 1 engines (R1, X26, X24, X23, X22). These classical engines operated between 2400 and 2600 Elo. 
Because the training labels were determined solely by the final game result:
- In games where an engine attained a $+300\text{ cp}$ advantage but blundered in the late middlegame and lost, every single position with an advantage was labeled as $0.0$ (Loss).
- This outcome-only labeling introduced severe **label noise**, penalizing sound tactical and positional moves.

### 5. Architectural Recommendations for Milestone P2.2+

1. **Incorporate Search Evaluations (Lambda Targets):**
   Combine final game outcome $z$ with deep alpha-beta search evaluation $q$:
   $$y_{\text{target}} = (1 - \lambda) \cdot z + \lambda \cdot \sigma\left(\frac{q}{S}\right)$$
   This immediately eliminates the noise of outcome-only labeling.
2. **Endgame Oversampling:**
   Augment the dataset with endgame positions from Nalimov/Syzygy tablebase positions or endgame suites to provide gradient signal on piece opposition and passed pawn conversion.
3. **Opening Book Cleansing:**
   Enforce a strict filter excluding positions before ply 16 to guarantee zero overlap with tournament opening test suites.
