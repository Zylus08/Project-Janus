## 1. Executive Summary & Core Scientific Findings

Milestone P1.11 was chartered to determine why Project Janus reached an apparent performance saturation plateau across Milestones P1.8 through P1.10, where extensive handcrafted evaluation terms (E10–E15) and search ordering enhancements failed to outperform the lean baselines **Janus Classical X7** (1T) and **Janus Classical X12** (4T).

Through rigorous counterfactual search analysis, evaluation compatibility testing, architecture decision recording, and 540 tournament games against internal baselines and unrestricted Stockfish 19, Milestone P1.11 established the following critical findings:

1. **Resolution of the "Parity" Myth (Phase 0 Audit):**
   Reconstruction of all 540 P1.10 games revealed that past claims of X15/X12 "parity" were statistical fallacies based on misunderstanding $p > 0.05$. Formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) failed ($p_{\text{TOST}} \ge 0.23$), proving that equivalence was never demonstrated.
2. **Identification of the Tactical Over-Pruning Defect (Phase 2 Audit):**
   Isolated counterfactual pruning search across 39 strategic and tactical positions revealed that **Late Move Reductions (LMR) caused a 33.33% move disagreement rate (13/39)**, disproportionately discarding quiet defensive refutations (`DEF_01` `h2h3`, `DEF_02` `f3e5`, `KNG_03` `e1g1`, `END_04` `d4d5`) before alpha-beta could verify their tactical soundness.
3. **Architectural Intervention (Direction A):**
   Candidate **Janus Classical X16** was engineered with **Evaluation-Aware Pruning Safety and History-Dampened LMR**:
   - Tracking static evaluation across plies in `g_ss[ply].static_eval` and computing the `improving` flag.
   - Expanding Reverse Futility Pruning (RFP) margins by $+40\times d$ when the position is not improving.
   - Dampening LMR by $-1$ ply when not improving or when continuation history is positive (`tot_h > 2000`).
   - Achieving **4,429,159 NPS** on 4 threads and restoring 25.64% of move decisions across held-out benchmark positions.
4. **Independent Strength Qualification Results (Phase 10):**
   - **Match A (1T vs X7):** X16-S scored 33.75% (-117.2 Elo, $p = 1.02 \times 10^{-7}$). X7 conclusively dominates single-threaded play due to minimal latency and branch simplicity.
   - **Match B (4T vs X12):** X16-P scored 45.50% (-31.4 Elo, $p = 0.1177$).
   - **Match C (4T Slow vs X12):** X16-P scored exactly 45.50% (-31.4 Elo, $p = 0.1921$).
   - TOST equivalence failed in all matches ($p_{\text{TOST}} \ge 0.43$).
5. **Stockfish 19 Calibration (Phase 11):**
   Candidate X16 successfully held **3 draws against unrestricted Stockfish 19** (2 draws on 1T, 1 draw on 4T), demonstrating improved tactical resilience over X15 (which held 0 draws). However, unrestricted Stockfish 19 with default NNUE retains a ~650–750 Elo superiority over pure handcrafted evaluation.
6. **Final Lineage Adjudication (Phase 12):**
   Under **Category C (Formal Baseline Retention)**:
   - **Janus Classical X7 is retained as the Qualified Single-Thread Classical Baseline.**
   - **Janus Classical X12 is retained as the Qualified Four-Thread Classical Baseline.**
   - Candidate X16 is archived as an experimental platform for pruning safety research.

## 4. Architectural Analysis: Why Handcrafted Classical Chess Saturates

The forensic evidence collected across P1.11 resolves the fundamental question of why classical engines plateau:
1. **The Evaluation Latency Tax:**
   Evaluating 15 complex handcrafted features (mobility, outposts, passer candidates, king pawn shields) slows down search throughput. In single-threaded execution, this reduction in raw nodes per second directly translates to a 1–2 ply tactical search depth deficit, explaining why X7 (-117 Elo) easily outcalculates X16-S.
2. **Non-Linear Term Interference:**
   Handcrafted chess evaluations lack mathematical orthogonalization. Adding a bonus for a knight outpost inadvertently distorts bishop mobility or king safety tradeoffs. Without large-scale automated tuning (SPSA / Texel tuning), human-crafted coefficients inevitably introduce positional noise.
3. **The Selective Search Tradeoff:**
   Aggressive pruning heuristics (NMP, RFP, LMP, LMR) are necessary to reach high search depths, but each introduces tactical blind spots. Direction A proved that pruning safety successfully rescues quiet refutations, but cannot compensate for the underlying evaluation inaccuracy.
