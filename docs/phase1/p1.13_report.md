## 1. Executive Summary & Milestone Verdict

Milestone P1.13 was chartered to resolve the fundamental architectural plateau observed across Project Janus: determining why iterative handcrafted evaluation and search interventions have repeatedly failed to translate into reproducible competitive playing strength.

Through the execution of **530 total tournament games** (120 historical calibration games and 410 controlled qualification games), comprehensive C++17 search profiling, an audit of 60 stratified decision positions, and an ablated classical candidate implementation (Janus Classical X18 with History-Modulated Late Move Reductions), this milestone delivers the following conclusive findings:

1. **The Core Search Diagnostic Discovery:**  
   Auditing 60 diverse opening, middlegame, tactical, and endgame test positions against Stockfish 11 revealed that **Janus does not primarily lose due to catastrophic tactical blunders** (Q1 was only 8.3%). Instead, **53.3% of Janus's losses stem from Quiet Horizon Instability (Q2, 30.0%) and Move Ordering Inversions (Q3, 23.3%)**.
2. **The Throughput–Selectivity Dilemma:**  
   Profiling demonstrated that Janus maintains high computational raw throughput (1.5M–2.5M NPS 1T, 5.5M–6.5M NPS 4T). However, Stockfish 11 achieves an effective branching factor of $b_{\text{eff}} \approx 1.4\text{--}1.8$, searching **8× to 28× fewer nodes** to reach equivalent nominal depths through sophisticated multi-ply continuation histories and dynamic pruning.
3. **The Implementation of Candidate X18:**  
   Candidate X18 introduced History-Modulated Late Move Reductions (History-LMR), preserving quiet moves with high positive history ($H > 8000 \to R-1$) and pruning quiet moves with negative history ($H < -2000 \to R+1$). In controlled development ablations, this achieved a **24.0% reduction in total search nodes** and a **36.1% reduction in search latency** on 4 threads.
4. **Independent Competitive Qualification (410 Games Total):**  
   - In Match 1 (1T, 1.0s + 50ms, 100 games), X18 scored **41.00%** ($-63.2$ Elo, 95% CI: $[-132.0, +5.5]$) against baseline X7.
   - In Match 2 (1T slow, 2.5s + 100ms, 50 games), X18 scored **43.00%** ($-49.0$ Elo, 95% CI: $[-145.0, +47.1]$) against baseline X7.
   - In Match 3 (4T, 1.0s + 50ms, 100 games), X18 scored **50.50%** ($+3.5$ Elo, 95% CI: $[-64.2, +71.1]$) against baseline X12.
   - In Matches 4–7 against external engines, X18 achieved 1 win and 1 draw against Stockfish 4 (3.75%), held 2 authentic threefold draws against Stockfish 19 at 1T (2.50%), held 2 authentic threefold draws against Stockfish 19 at 4T (2.50%), and was held to 0/40 against Stockfish 11.
5. **The Final Statutory Decision:**  
   Under the strict JCTS qualification criteria, Candidate X18 **fails** the statutory requirement for baseline adoption (Category A requires statistically significant superiority, $p < 0.05$, lower CI bound $> 50.0\%$). Candidate X18 is classified as **Category C (Hypothesis Rejected)** and permanently archived.  
   **Janus Classical X7 (`b34e342`) is retained as the single-thread qualified baseline.**  
   **Janus Classical X12 (`78ca3d5`) is retained as the four-thread qualified baseline.**

## 8. Forensic Root-Cause Analysis: The Mechanism of Regression

Why did an intervention that reduced nodes by 24% fail in match play?
1. **The Butterfly History Noise Problem:** Butterfly history tables ($64 \times 64$) track piece-from to piece-to cutoffs globally. At shallow single-threaded depths ($d \approx 7\text{--}9$), history scores are sparse and noisy.
2. **Asymmetric Pruning Penalty:** Tactically critical quiet defensive moves that failed once in another subtree received negative history penalties ($H < -2000$). X18 consequently reduced them by an extra ply ($R = R_{\text{base}} + 1$), pushing defensive refutations beyond the horizon.
3. **SMP Diversity Masking:** In Lazy SMP, diverse helper thread search offsets compensated for thread-0 pruning errors, masking the tactical vulnerability and achieving 50.5% at 4T. Single-threaded search exposed the unbuffered vulnerability.
