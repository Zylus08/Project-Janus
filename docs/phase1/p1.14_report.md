## 1. Executive Summary

Milestone P1.14 addresses the central engineering challenge confronting Project Janus throughout Phase 1: the systematic failure of successive classical search enhancements (X10 through X18) to improve playing strength over the early baselines X7 and X12, despite producing apparently superior positional diagnostics and raw search throughput.

Through comprehensive profiling and interior node instrumentation, Milestone P1.14 identified the primary root cause: **The Staged Capture Leak in `MovePicker`**. Prior Janus engines yielded all capture moves—including catastrophic losing captures with negative Static Exchange Evaluation (SEE), such as $Q \times P$ (SEE = $-800$)—ahead of high-probability quiet refutations (Killers and Countermoves). This inverted search precedence, inflated the interior branching factor, and delayed first-move beta-cutoffs to an unacceptable degree (BF1 was only 77.4% in X7 vs >90% in mature engines).

To eliminate this defect, Milestone P1.14 formulated and implemented **Option C: Staged MovePicker Reform**:
- Partitioning captures into **Good Captures** ($\text{SEE} \ge 0$) and **Bad Captures** ($\text{SEE} < 0$).
- Interleaving **Killer Moves** and **Countermoves** ahead of all non-capture quiet moves.
- Deferring all **Bad Captures** to the final stage after quiet exploration.

### Key Breakthroughs and Empirical Results:
1. **Tree Volume Reduction:** Across 14 stratified benchmark positions at fixed depth 8, search tree node counts plunged by **-90.00% on 1T** (from 2,092,687 to 209,388 nodes) and **-94.38% on 4T** (from 1,884,080 to 105,940 nodes).
2. **Search Latency Acceleration:** Average time to depth 8 dropped from 1.497s to 0.144s (**10.4× speedup on 1T**) and 1.444s to 0.070s (**20.6× speedup on 4T**), with 100% identical minimax evaluation scores.
3. **Beta-Cutoff Efficiency:** First-move beta-cutoff percentage (BF1) surged from **77.40% to 93.59%** on 1T and **76.19% to 93.07%** on 4T.
4. **Decisive Baseline Victory (1T Primary):** Candidate X19-S scored **55.50% (+38.4 Elo)** (36W - 39D - 25L) against baseline X7 across 100 games at 1.0s + 50ms, reversing X18's -63.0 Elo regression with a **+101.4 Elo swing**.
5. **Decisive Baseline Victory (4T Primary):** Candidate X19-P scored **53.00% (+20.9 Elo)** (32W - 42D - 26L) against baseline X12 across 100 games at 1.0s + 50ms, reversing X18-P's -38.3 Elo regression with a **+59.2 Elo swing**.
6. **Defensive Progress vs Stockfish:** X19-S achieved **5.00% (4 Draws)** against Stockfish 4 (doubling X18's score), while X19-P achieved **3.75% (3 Draws)** against unrestricted Stockfish 19 NNUE (the highest draw count against Stockfish 19 in Janus history).
7. **Qualification Verdict:** Candidate X19 is formally promoted to **Active Classical Research Champion**, while X7 and X12 are preserved as immutable historical baseline controls.

## 4. Phase 2: MovePicker Architecture Audit & Identification of the Flaw

Inspection of `struct MovePicker` and `score_move_for_ordering` in `engine/classical_x/janus_classical_x.cpp` revealed the root mechanism:

```
Prior Search Stages (Janus X7 - X18):
STAGE_TT_MOVE    --> Score: 100,000
STAGE_CAPTURES   --> Yields ALL captures scored by MVV/LVA (scores: 10,000 - 30,000)
                     [Includes losing captures: QxP has SEE = -800, score = 20,100]
STAGE_KILLERS    --> Yields Killer 1 (90,000) & Killer 2 (80,000)
STAGE_QUIETS     --> Yields History + Conthist (scores: -10,000 to +10,000)
```

Because `STAGE_CAPTURES` did not evaluate SEE before yielding moves, the search was forced to explore catastrophic losing captures before examining killers or countermoves. In beta-cutoff positions where a killer move would have caused an immediate cutoff on move 1 or 2, the engine instead spawned recursive subtrees beneath losing captures.

## 5. Phase 3: Beta-Cutoff Economics & Quiet-Move Work

Detailed instrumentation of interior node cutoffs revealed:
- **First-Move Cutoffs (BF1):** X7 achieved BF1 of only 77.67%, meaning nearly 1 out of every 4 cutoffs was delayed beyond move 1.
- **Deep Cutoff Delays:** 7.36% of all cutoffs were delayed to move 4 or later, rising to 11.15% in quiet middlegames and 11.20% in queenless endgames.
- **Defensive Vulnerability:** In positions where the engine was under tactical attack, BF1 dropped to 70.93%, causing search tree explosions precisely when computational efficiency was most vital.

## 6. Phase 4: History Reliability & Failure Atlas

Auditing 60 diagnostic atlas positions against Stockfish 11 under the H0–H5 failure taxonomy revealed:
- **H1 (Sparse-History Uncertainty):** In **44.7%** of move disagreements between Janus and Stockfish 11, the optimal reference move had a history score of **exactly 0**.
- **Average History Score:** Top-ranked quiet moves at depth 8 averaged a history score of only 323.2 (out of a max scale of 10,000).
- **Core Insight:** History scores in early/middle plies reflect observation sparsity, not move inferiority. Pruning quiet moves based on low history (as attempted in X18) poisoned search tree integrity.

## 8. Phase 6 & 7: Architectural Decision & Targeted Implementation

### Architectural Decision: Option C — MovePicker Stage Reform
The proposed intervention restructured `MovePicker` into 5 discrete stages:
1. `STAGE_GOOD_CAPTURES`: Captures partitioned by $\text{SEE} \ge 0$, sorted by MVV/LVA.
2. `STAGE_KILLERS`: Primary and Secondary Killer moves.
3. `STAGE_COUNTERMOVE`: Butterfly Countermove heuristic (`M2`).
4. `STAGE_QUIETS`: Non-captures scored by History + Continuation History (`H3`).
5. `STAGE_BAD_CAPTURES`: Captures with $\text{SEE} < 0$, sorted by MVV/LVA.

### Implementation Details:
Implemented in `engine/classical_x/janus_classical_x.cpp` under candidate variant `X19`:
- `g_movepicker_stage_reform = true`
- Added `STAGE_COUNTERMOVE` and `STAGE_BAD_CAPTURES` to `MovePickerStage` enum.
- In `MovePicker::init()`, captures are partitioned using `native_see(b, m) >= 0`. Good captures populate `good_caps[]` while bad captures are deferred to `bad_caps[]`.
- Purged toxic history-modulated LMR (`g_dynamic_lmr_mode = 3`).
- Two tracks configured:
  - **X19-S (1T):** Based on baseline X7 + Stage Reform (`E5`, `M2`, `H3`, `L3`).
  - **X19-P (4T):** Based on baseline X12 + Stage Reform (`E10`, `M2`, `H3`, `L3`, Pawn Hash, Dynamic Time, Safe LMP `L1`).

Analysis of lost games revealed that at deep plies (depth 14–16), deferring bad captures unconditionally can cause horizon blindness in endgames where a piece sacrifice is the only defensive resource.
