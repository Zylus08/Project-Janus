1. Resides on a half-open file (no friendly pawns ahead) or closed file.

## 7. Forensic Analysis: Why King-File Exposure (Track B) Failed

Milestone P1.18's Track B attempted to penalize open and semi-open files facing the king ($-15$ to $-40\text{ mg}$). Forensic re-analysis of Dev-3 games in Phase 7 revealed four structural failure modes:
1. **Premature Shield Advances:** When an enemy rook occupied an open file on the flank, Janus pushed its own pawns forward (e.g. g2-g4 or h2-h4) to "close" the file, irrevocably destroying its king shelter and allowing immediate tactical invasion.
2. **Suppressed Counterplay:** The engine refused to open files for its own rooks if the file opened anywhere near its own king.
3. **Lack of Dynamic Infiltration Detection:** The term penalized files even when the opponent possessed no rooks or queens capable of using the file.
4. **Search Horizon Aspiration Volatility:** The sudden penalty applied when a file opened triggered aspiration search window failures, increasing node waste by 18%.

Milestone P1.19 completely excised Track B, recovering +28.1 Elo in development play.

## 10. External Benchmark Calibration & The "NNUE Paradox"

### 10.1 The Paradox Explained
Matches 5–7 reveal a striking empirical phenomenon:
- Candidate X24 scored **0.0%** against Stockfish 4 (2014) and **2.5%** against Stockfish 11 (2020).
- Yet it scored **6.25%** (5 draws in 40 games) against Stockfish 19 NNUE (2026).

Forensic analysis of the 5 draws against Stockfish 19 shows that at fast blitz (1.5s + 50ms), NNUE evaluations in locked, closed pawn structures often settle into small static advantages (+0.4 to +0.8 cp) without attempting radical pawn sacrifices. When Janus maintains a compact defensive shell, the game reaches threefold repetition or the 50-move limit.

In stark contrast, mature classical engines (SF4 and SF11) possess handcrafted mobility and pawn-break terms that ruthlessly sacrifice pawns to shatter closed structures, overwhelming Janus tactically in endgames.
