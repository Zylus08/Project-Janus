## 1. Executive Summary & Core Scientific Breakthrough

Milestone P1.21 was chartered to resolve the central dilemma of late Phase 1 classical development: **Why did successive classical candidates (X23, X24, X25) fail to outperform older frozen baselines, particularly at longer time controls?**

Through sixteen systematically executed phases—combining unified source diffing, counterfactual pruning audits, evaluation perturbation studies, depth-dependent reliability profiling, shallow exact reference minimax verification, search-cost microbenchmarking, controlled ablations, full perft/referee correctness verification, and an authoritative 500-game tournament battery—the milestone delivered definitive, incontrovertible answers.

### Key Milestones & Findings:
1. **Search-Code Bitwise Identity (Phase 1):** Line-by-line diff analysis proved that Candidate X24 (P1.19) and Candidate X25 (P1.20) share **$100\%$ bitwise identical search routines** with Reference X22 (P1.17). All behavioral and competitive divergence was causally driven by static evaluation interaction with search pruning.
2. **Reverse Futility Pruning Contract Defects (Phase 2):** Auditing the search contract revealed four major implementation defects in Janus's Reverse Futility Pruning (RFP):
   - RFP pruned unconditionally at PV nodes (`beta - alpha > 1`), corrupting principal variations.
   - RFP executed `return static_eval;` without storing a `TT_LOWER` bound in the transposition table.
   - RFP ignored existing TT upper bounds proving the position fails low.
   - RFP pruned in pure pawn endgames without checking non-pawn material (zugzwang risk).
3. **The Pruning Paradox Discovered (Phase 10 & 13):**
   - Option A was implemented in Candidate X26, perfectly repairing all four RFP contract defects.
   - In controlled ablations (Phase 10), Option A cured tactical discovery latency (immediately finding winning piece sacrifice `1. Nxe6!` at Depth 4 in `CF_T3`) and refined root moves in quiet positions (`f1d3!` in `CF_Q2`).
   - However, in timed match play (Phase 13, 400 games), **Candidate X26 scored only $37.0\%\text{–}42.5\%$ against the legacy baselines (X7, X12, X22)**.
   - **Scientific Attribution:** The legacy baselines' buggy RFP contract pruned PV and quiet branches hyper-aggressively, compressing search trees by $20\%\text{–}60\%$. Under a clock, this artificial compression yielded a **$1\text{ to }2$ ply nominal search depth advantage** over X26. In computer chess, nominal depth trumps selective purity under timed match play.
4. **Hard Ceiling of the Classical Paradigm Reached (Phase 15):**
   - Handcrafted classical evaluation functions cannot be tuned further without destabilizing search pruning thresholds, while repairing search pruning reduces effective depth.
   - Candidate X26 is **formally rejected** as a qualified strength successor.
   - Frozen baselines **Janus Classical X7 (`b34e342`)** (1T) and **Janus Classical X12 (`78ca3d5`)** (4T) are reaffirmed as the qualified strength controls.
   - Milestone P1.22 is chartered as the **final classical milestone**, executing a clean-sheet search redesign and evaluation decoupling to conclude Phase 1.

- Documented controlling inequality: $\text{StaticEval} - \text{Margin}(d) \ge \beta$ where $\text{Margin}(d) = 120 \times d$ (1T) or $90 \times d$ (4T).
- Uncovered four structural defects: missing Non-PV guard, missing TT write, TT upper-bound contradiction, and pure pawn endgame zugzwang vulnerability.

### Phase 3: Counterfactual Pruning Decision Audit
- Executed clean-state RFP ON vs RFP OFF across 12 positions at depths 2–5 (48 paired runs).
- Move agreement was $95.8\%$ (46/48). Discovered tactical discovery latency in `CF_T3` (Scheveningen Attack), where RFP ON missed winning knight sacrifice `d4e6` ($+155\text{ cp}$) at Depth 4, while RFP OFF found it immediately.

### Phase 4: Static Evaluation Perturbation Study
- Implemented diagnostic score perturbation toggle (`UCI_SyntheticPerturbation`) in `janus_classical_x.cpp`.
- Evaluated 16 positions at Depth 6 across E0, E1, E2, E3.
- Discovered that in `P01_SIC_D6`, E1 (backward pawns) caused a **$+59.5\%$ node explosion** (95,073 nodes vs E0's 59,605 nodes) and flipped root move, while E2 (connected passers) matched E0 perfectly (59,614 nodes). Classified 5 distinct search-eval interaction types.

### Phase 5: Depth-Dependent Search Reliability
- Evaluated 9 provenance-tracked archetypes across depths 4–8 for E0, E1, E2.
- Proved that E1 diverged repeatedly in quiet structures to avoid opening files near backward pawns, suffering a $+36.4\%$ node inflation by Depth 8. E2 maintained exact root move stability across all depths.

### Phase 6: Shallow Exact Search Reference Verification

- RFP is the dominant forward pruning operator, executing cutoffs on **$41.6\%\text{–}73.2\%$ of probed nodes** at Depth 8 in tactical positions.
