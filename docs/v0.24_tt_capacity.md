### 1. Motivation and Experimental Objective

In Milestone v0.23, the baseline benchmark on 6 standard positions occupied fewer than 1% of the 200,000 TT entry budget (~1,000–3,800 total stores across all positions). Consequently, capacity overflow (`tt_clears`) was never triggered during normal searches.

The objective of Milestone v0.24 Phase 2 is to intentionally induce severe capacity pressure and compare `always_replace`, `depth_preferred`, and `two_tier` across three distinct capacity budgets:
1. **Small Budget ($1{,}000$ entries)**: Heavy capacity pressure where single-position search trees exceed table capacity, forcing active evictions.
2. **Medium Budget ($5{,}000$ entries)**: Moderate capacity budget comfortably accommodating individual position trees (~1,040 peak retained entries) while constraining multi-position accumulation.
3. **Standard Budget ($200{,}000$ entries)**: The unconstrained baseline.

---

### 2. Experimental Methodology

- **Workload**: 6 standard benchmark positions (`benchmarks/positions.json`) searched to depth 5.
- **Controls**:
  - PVS, Aspiration Windows (+/-35 cp), LMR, NMP all enabled.
  - SEE ordering and SEE pruning disabled (`False`).
  - Check extensions disabled (`"disabled"`).
  - 3 interleaved repetitions per configuration with deterministic random shuffle (`seed = 20261017`).
- **Telemetry Tracked**:
  - Total nodes, median elapsed time, min/max elapsed time.
  - Probes, hits, hit rate %, usable hits, cutoffs, cutoff rate %.
  - Stores, same-key updates, same-key overwrites, same-key rejected.
  - Table clears (`tt_clears`) and maximum retained entries.
  - PV legality and move/score agreement across all positions.

### 5. Key Findings and Analysis

1. **Thrashing in Partitioned Architectures under Extreme Constraints**:
   - Under the $1{,}000$-entry budget, `two_tier` partitions storage into two $500$-entry tables (`tt_deep` and `tt_recent`).
   - Because single tactical positions generate over $1{,}000$ stores, each $500$-entry tier fills prematurely, triggering $4$ table clears (compared to $1$ in `always_replace` and `depth_preferred`).
   - Consequently, `two_tier` suffered an $11.0\%$ reduction in hit rate ($43.21\%$ vs $50.78\%$) and a $+8.09\%$ node penalty ($58{,}645$ vs $54{,}253$ nodes).
2. **Convergence at Practical Capacities ($\ge 5{,}000$ entries)**:
   - At $5{,}000$ entries and above, peak search tree occupancy per position ($\approx 1{,}040$ entries) never triggers table overflow.
   - `always_replace` yields $53{,}707$ nodes, while `depth_preferred` and `two_tier` yield $53{,}803$ nodes ($+0.18\%$).
   - Best-move agreement remained $100\%$ across all 6 positions and all capacity budgets.
   - All generated Principal Variations were verified to be $100\%$ legal chess moves.
3. **Correctness Under Pressure**:
   - Targeted unit tests in `tests/test_tt_capacity.py` verified that same-key updates at deeper plies succeed without clearing, shallower updates are rejected without corrupting deep entries, and table clearing correctly purges both tiers.

---

### 6. Conclusion and Default Policy Verdict

- **Capacity Recommendation**: The standard default of $200{,}000$ entries (~18–24 MB) provides an enormous safety margin against premature table eviction for depths 3–6.
- **Replacement Policy Verdict**: Under tight memory constraints, `always_replace` and `depth_preferred` avoid the tier partitioning penalty of `two_tier`. In standard memory configurations, all three policies perform essentially identically.
- Therefore, retaining `always_replace` as the production default remains sound, with `depth_preferred` preserved as a robust alternative.
