### 1. Executive Summary

Milestone v0.26 executed a systematic factorial ablation study to isolate and evaluate the independent and combined effects of **Static Exchange Evaluation (SEE) move ordering** and **SEE-based quiescence pruning**.

In Milestone v0.25, the combined configuration (both ordering and pruning enabled) achieved an exact 50.0% score against baseline across 100 games at fixed depth 3. The hypothesis was raised that conservative quiescence pruning might have suppressed tactical sacrifices, thereby neutralizing an otherwise positive effect from SEE move ordering. Milestone v0.26 resolved this hypothesis by executing:
1. **Factorial Audit & Implementation Correction**: Verified exact code execution paths; corrected prior documentation mislabeling SEE ordering as evaluating "quiet exchanges".
2. **Phase I Tactical Audit**: 144 evaluations across 12 positions at depths 3, 4, 5 with 100% legal moves, 100% legal PVs, 100% board immutability, and 100% forced mate detection across all 4 arms.
3. **Phase II Factorial Performance Benchmark**: 360 runs (6 canonical positions $\times$ 4 arms $\times$ 3 depths $\times$ 5 interleaved/rotated repetitions), measuring main effects and interaction effects.
4. **Phase IV Playing-Strength Validation**: A 100-game paired match at fixed depth 3 with 128 MB Hash evaluating **Arm B (Ordering-only)** against **Arm A (Baseline)** across 50 opening pairs with color reversal.

#### Key Match Findings (100 Games, Depth 3, 128 MB Hash):
- **Aggregate Result**: Exactly **50.0 / 100 points (50.0%)** for both Control (`Janus-Baseline`) and Treatment (`Janus-SEE-Ordering`).
- **Record**: **23 Wins, 54 Draws, 23 Losses** for both engines.
- **Paired Unit Outcome (50 Opening Pairs)**:
  - Pairs won by Treatment: **0**
  - Pairs tied: **50**
  - Pairs lost by Treatment: **0**
  - Mean Paired Difference: **+0.00%** ($\text{SE} = \pm 0.00\%$)
  - Paired $t$-test ($df = 49$): $t = 0.0000$, $p = 1.00000$
  - Parametric 95% Confidence Interval: **[0.00%, 0.00%]**
  - Bootstrap 95% Confidence Interval: **[0.00%, 0.00%]**
- **Definitive Conclusion**: The hypothesis that quiescence pruning was masking an underlying benefit of SEE capture ordering is **falsified**. Even with quiescence pruning completely disabled, SEE capture ordering alone produces zero net playing-strength advantage under fixed depth search.
- **Final Recommendation: Category B — No clear strength improvement established.** Retain production defaults with both SEE ordering and pruning **disabled (`False`)**.

### 2. Implementation Audit & Documentation Correction

#### Audit of Previous Descriptions
Earlier project documentation (including the v0.25 report) occasionally used the colloquial phrase "optimizing move ordering for quiet exchanges." An audit of the codebase confirms that this description was technically inaccurate:
- **Quiet moves are never evaluated by SEE**: In `_move_order_score` (`src/janus/search.py`, line 503), the condition is `if board.is_capture(move):`. Non-capturing quiet moves bypass the entire SEE block and are ordered exclusively by killer heuristic slots (700k / 600k) and the history table.
- **Actual SEE Ordering Behavior**:
  - Winning and equal captures (`see_val >= 0`) receive a bonus of `1_000_000 + see_val * 100 + mvv_lva`, ensuring they are ordered ahead of killers and quiet history moves.
  - Losing captures (`see_val < 0`) are demoted to `50_000 + see_val * 10 + mvv_lva`, placing them below killer moves and below high-scoring quiet history moves.
- **Actual SEE Pruning Behavior**:
  - In `_quiescence` (`src/janus/search.py`, line 620), when not in check and `enable_see_pruning = True`:
  - Promotion moves (`move.promotion is not None`) and checking captures (`board.gives_check(move)`) are **strictly exempted** from pruning.
  - Only non-checking, non-promotion captures with `see_val < 0` are pruned (`continue`).

#### Critical Factorial Insights:
1. **Computational Overhead of Ordering**: In all depths, the percentage wall-clock speedup of Ordering-only (Arm B) was substantially smaller than its percentage node reduction (-19.1% nodes $\to$ -11.5% time at depth 3; -12.9% nodes $\to$ -3.97% time at depth 5). This reflects the CPU overhead of evaluating the Static Exchange Evaluation minimax tree across thousands of captures.
2. **Efficiency of Pruning**: Pruning-only (Arm C) achieved superior wall-clock efficiency because it only invokes SEE on captures in quiescence search that are not promotions or checks (1,038 calls at depth 3 vs 5,953 in Arm B).
3. **Sub-Additive Interaction**: The interaction effect is positive for node counts across all depths (+6.5% to +12.4%). Ordering and pruning exhibit diminishing returns: when captures are already pruned, re-ordering them saves fewer nodes, and when captures are already ordered favorably, pruning the tail cuts fewer branches.

### 10. Final Recommendation and Implementation Policy

Under the Project Janus Decision Framework:
- **Verdict: Category B — No clear strength improvement established.**
- **Production Defaults**: **Retain `enable_see_ordering = False` and `enable_see_pruning = False` as the production defaults.**
- **Ablation Synthesis**:
  1. SEE capture ordering saves 13%–24% of search nodes, but incurs significant computational overhead in Python, resulting in modest wall-clock gains (4%–14%).
  2. SEE quiescence pruning saves 14%–25% of nodes with less computational overhead.
  3. However, under fixed depth search, neither ordering alone, pruning alone, nor combined search alters move quality or final game outcomes against the baseline.
  4. Both options remain fully verified, independently configurable UCI options (`SEE_Ordering` and `SEE_Pruning`) and CLI arguments (`--see-ordering`, `--see-pruning`).
- **Future Direction**: Playing-strength enhancements should focus on move generation vectorization, search generation aging, and dynamic time management rather than further capture pruning heuristics.
