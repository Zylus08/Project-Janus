   In a matched, same-environment 5-repetition interleaved experiment executed under identical Python 3.11 conditions:
   - **Depth 4 Suite Wall-Clock Time:** Fell from 1.716s to 1.400s (**+18.4% Ratio of Medians speedup**, **+19.0% paired speedup median**, saving 0.325s per pass).
   - **Depth 5 Suite Wall-Clock Time:** Fell from 3.087s to 2.402s (**+22.2% Ratio of Medians speedup**, **+21.4% paired speedup median**, saving 0.680s per pass).
   - **Throughput (NPS):** Increased from 15,390 NPS to **18,864 NPS (+22.6%)** at depth 4, and from 17,398 NPS to **22,360 NPS (+28.5%)** at depth 5.
   - **Uniform Improvement:** Every single non-terminal benchmark position showed a consistent +16% to +30% speedup across all repetitions.

3. **Correction of the v0.30 Reporting Anomaly (`white_up_rook` D4):**
   The v0.30 report displayed `Base Med: 0.249s | Cand Med: 0.325s | Speedup: +6.4%`. This apparent contradiction was caused by computing the **median of paired speedups** ($+6.4\%$) across the 5 repetitions (where 3 of 5 runs favored candidate) while displaying the **marginal medians** ($0.249\text{s}$ vs $0.325\text{s}$, a $-30.5\%$ ratio of medians) driven by a single transient spike in Repetition 5. This audit provides clear, dual-metric reporting rules to avoid misleading presentation.

4. **Resolution of the Aggregate Metric Drift in v0.30:**
   In v0.28 and v0.29, suite aggregates were computed as the total wall-clock time to execute all 6 positions in a repetition ($\sum_{p} T_p$). In v0.30, the analysis script pooled all 30 single-position runs into an unweighted list, reporting an overall median of $+8.0\%$. Because sub-second tactical/terminal positions (`stalemate`, `promotion_tactic`, `mate_in_one`) exhibited high percentage gains, they outvoted the complex middlegame (`open_game`, which accounts for $>60\%$ of total search runtime). The true workload-weighted suite total speedup for v0.30 relative to v0.29 was **+0.3% at D4** and **+1.7% at D5** (matching cProfile's attribution of ~0.38s saved in quiescence search).

5. **Resolution of the Historical Baseline Timing Discrepancy:**
   The apparent variation in raw baseline execution time across milestone runs (v0.28 ~3.2s, v0.29 ~11.1s, v0.30 ~8.9s) was not an engine regression or algorithmic change. The underlying search node counts were completely invariant (53,707 nodes). The variation was entirely driven by Windows CPU frequency scaling, power state transitions, and workstation load. Interleaving within each run protected internal comparisons, but cross-milestone conclusions require matched re-benchmarking as performed in this audit.

### 4. Corrected Arithmetic & Aggregation Methodology

To eliminate reporting ambiguity and statistical discrepancies, Project Janus adopts the following explicit definitions:

#### A. Standard Speedup Formula
$$\text{Speedup (\%)} = 100 \times \left(1 - \frac{T_{\text{candidate}}}{T_{\text{baseline}}}\right)$$
- **Positive (+):** Candidate is faster.
- **Negative (-):** Candidate is slower (regression).

#### B. Distinction Between Marginal Medians and Paired Speedup
When evaluating paired benchmark repetitions:
1. **Marginal Medians:** $T_{\text{base, med}} = \text{Median}(\{T_{\text{base}, i}\})$ and $T_{\text{cand, med}} = \text{Median}(\{T_{\text{cand}, i}\})$.
   $$\text{Ratio of Medians Speedup (\%)} = 100 \times \left(1 - \frac{T_{\text{cand, med}}}{T_{\text{base, med}}}\right)$$
2. **Paired Speedup Distribution:** For each repetition $i \in \{1 \dots N\}$:
   $$S_i = 100 \times \left(1 - \frac{T_{\text{cand}, i}}{T_{\text{base}, i}}\right)$$
   $$\text{Paired Speedup Median (\%)} = \text{Median}(\{S_i\})$$
3. **Mandatory Reporting Rule:**
   If marginal medians are displayed, the speedup column must explicitly state whether it is `RatioOfMed` or `PairedMed`. Both must be reported whenever the two diverge in sign or magnitude by $>5\%$.

#### C. Suite Total Aggregation (Workload-Weighted Sum)
To prevent fast sub-second positions from dominating the aggregate:
$$T_{\text{suite}, i} = \sum_{p=1}^{P} T_{p, i}$$
The suite speedup is evaluated from the suite totals $T_{\text{suite}, i}$, weighting each position proportionally to the actual compute cycles it demands.
