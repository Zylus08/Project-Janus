### 1. Executive Summary

Milestone v0.32 conducts a direct, empirical playing-strength validation to answer the primary architectural question raised by the v0.31 performance audit:  
**Does the ~22% cumulative search throughput speedup achieved across v0.28–v0.30 translate into a measurable playing-strength improvement when both engines search under identical fixed-time conditions?**

To answer this question, a 100-game, colour-balanced, paired opening match was executed between pre-optimisation baseline **Janus v0.27** (`851afc7`) and candidate **Janus v0.30** (`05de411`) under a fixed time control of **500 ms per move** (`movetime 500ms`) across all 50 canonical opening positions from [`benchmarks/openings.json`](file:///e:/ChessXML/project-janus/benchmarks/openings.json).

#### Primary Findings & Conclusions

1. **Outcome: No Statistically Significant Playing-Strength Difference:**
   - Candidate v0.30 scored **47.0%** (**47.0 / 100 points**: 23 Wins, 48 Draws, 29 Losses).
   - **95% Wald Confidence Interval:** **[39.9%, 54.1%]**
   - **95% Paired Bootstrap Confidence Interval:** **[40.0%, 54.5%]**
   - **Estimated $\Delta\text{Elo}$:** **-20.9** (sampling standard error: $\pm 25.4$ Elo).
   - Because $50.0\%$ (true parity) lies well within the 95% confidence interval ($z = -0.60$, $p \approx 0.55$), the hypothesis that v0.30 has superior playing strength under fixed time is **rejected**. The two versions perform at statistical equivalence.

2. **Search Depth Differential Verified:**
   - Candidate v0.30 reached an average completed depth of **3.25 plies** per move, compared to **2.99 plies** for baseline v0.27 (**+0.26 plies deeper on average**).
   - Both engines adhered strictly to the fixed time limit: Candidate averaged **0.5153s** per move, Baseline averaged **0.5188s** per move (operating system and UCI process latency overhead $< 19\text{ms}$).
   - The ~22% throughput increase reliably manifested as additional search depth, confirming that the engine's search speedup functioned as intended in real games.

3. **Colour and Opening Symmetry:**
   - Candidate v0.30 scored identically as White and Black:
     - As White: **23.5 / 50 (47.0%)** (12W - 23D - 15L)
     - As Black: **23.5 / 50 (47.0%)** (11W - 25D - 14L)
   - Pair-level outcomes: **15 Pairs Won, 16 Pairs Drawn, 19 Pairs Lost** out of 50 completed pairs.
   - Match terminations: **52 Checkmates (52.0%)**, **48 Threefold Repetitions (48.0%)**.
   - Zero crashes, zero illegal moves, zero timeouts, and zero aborted games across all 100 games.

4. **Architectural Insight: The Shallow-Search Horizon Barrier:**
   Why did searching +0.26 plies deeper not yield Elo gains?
   - At shallow search depths (depth ~3) with a basic piece-value and center-bonus evaluation function, an incremental fractional depth increase (reaching depth 4 on some branches while remaining at depth 3 on others) does not provide systematic tactical superiority.
   - Without positional evaluation features (king safety, pawn structure, piece activity, mobility), shallow tactical depth increases often reveal tactical threats without providing the positional understanding required to convert slight advantages.
   - Therefore, future performance gains in Project Janus cannot rely solely on micro-optimizing search speed; engineering effort must shift toward **evaluation quality and search selectivity**.

### 2. Experimental Methodology & Control Configuration

#### A. Engine Binaries & Process Isolation
- **Baseline (`Janus-v0.27`):** Spawned from an isolated temporary Git worktree checked out to commit `851afc77ba3c05a77834b31a8fde551b407d5a90`, with an independent `PYTHONPATH` pointing strictly to v0.27 source files.
- **Candidate (`Janus-v0.30`):** Spawned from workspace commit `05de4112c27ec6b2f36a9c3be29b47a993a0a7dc`.
- Both engines operated as independent operating system processes communicating with the match harness strictly via standard UCI protocol (`python-chess.engine.SimpleEngine.popen_uci`).
- Identical Transposition Table allocation: **128 MB** per engine.
- Single-threaded execution (`Threads = 1`).
- `ucinewgame` sent before every game to ensure clean transposition tables, history tables, and killer slots.

#### B. Opening Suite & Colour Pairing
- 50 opening positions from [`benchmarks/openings.json`](file:///e:/ChessXML/project-janus/benchmarks/openings.json) (covering diverse ECO opening families).
- Deterministic opening selection with random seed `42`.
- Exact colour reversal: For each opening, Game 1 was played with Candidate as White, and Game 2 was played with Candidate as Black.
- Maximum game plies cap: 200 plies.

### 7. Why Search Speedup Did Not Translate Into Playing Strength

The empirical result (47.0% score despite +0.26 plies deeper search) provides crucial architectural guidance for Project Janus:

1. **The Tactical Horizon at Shallow Depth:**  
   In iterative deepening, searching +0.26 plies deeper means the engine completes depth 3 and searches a fraction of depth 4. At depth 3, the engine has a very shallow tactical horizon (3 half-moves). Reaching depth 4 on some branches can occasionally expose tactical threats, but without quiescence search depth or positional pruning, it can also lead to horizon-shifting traps where an engine misjudges trades.

2. **Evaluation Simplicity Outweighs Shallow Depth:**  
   Janus currently uses a basic material + center bonus evaluation function. In chess engine development, the general rule of thumb is:
   $$\text{Evaluation Quality} \gg \text{Fractional Depth Gain at Shallow Horizon}$$
   A +22% search speedup provides ~15–20 Elo in mature engines with sophisticated evaluation (tapered eval, pawn structures, piece-square tables, mobility, king safety). In an engine with simple evaluation, random tactical noise at depth 3 easily obscures an expected ~15 Elo difference.

3. **Statistical Power Limitations:**  
   In a 100-game match, the 95% confidence interval is $\pm 7.1\%$. To reliably detect a theoretical +15 Elo advantage (~52% win rate), a sample size of at least 1,000–2,000 games would be required. The 100-game match conclusively proves that v0.30 does not possess a *large* playing-strength advantage ($\ge +50\text{ Elo}$), and is practically equivalent to v0.27.
