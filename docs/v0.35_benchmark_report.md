## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.35 introduces an adaptive time management subsystem designed to support fixed-movetime, classical clock-only, and Fischer increment time controls without modifying the established v0.34 calibrated tapered evaluation.

This benchmark evaluates:
1. **Time-Allocation Latency:** Pure microsecond overhead of evaluating `TimeManager.allocate_time(...)`.
2. **Deadline Adherence and Overshoot:** Discrepancy between requested time budget and actual search duration across varying time limits and position complexities.
3. **Search Performance:** Nodes searched, search depth, and NPS across distinct operational regimes.

The results confirm that allocation logic introduces negligible overhead ($7.0\ \mu\text{s}$ to $21.0\ \mu\text{s}$) and that deadline checks (every 128 nodes plus boundary checks between iterative-deepening iterations) bound maximum deadline overshoot to $14.33\text{ ms}$, comfortably within the $50\text{ ms}$ safety reserve.

## 4. Analysis and Architectural Observations

### 4.1 Time-Allocation Latency
The mathematical calculation performed by `TimeManager.allocate_time` takes between $6.95\ \mu\text{s}$ and $20.95\ \mu\text{s}$. This represents $< 0.02\text{ ms}$, ensuring that allocating time has virtually zero measurable impact on search throughput or move decision overhead.

### 4.2 Deadline Observance and Overshoot
Across all 60 empirical trials:
- **Minimum Overshoot:** $0.00\text{ ms}$ (perfect deadline hit).
- **Median Overshoot:** Ranging from $0.00\text{ ms}$ to $14.33\text{ ms}$.
- **Worst-Case Overshoot:** $14.33\text{ ms}$ on `fischer_10s_plus_200ms`.
Because the engine configures a safety buffer $R_{\text{safe}} = 50\text{ ms}$, the observed worst-case overshoot of $14.33\text{ ms}$ consumes only $28.7\%$ of the safety margin. No game in any trial experienced an unexpected timeout or clock forfeit.

### 4.3 Low-Clock Emergency Handling
Under severe time constraints (`500 ms remaining + 50 ms increment`), the adaptive manager allocated $57.50\text{ ms}$. The engine searched 1,280 nodes, reaching depth 3 and terminating cleanly in $62.5\text{ ms}$ with an overshoot of just $5.0\text{ ms}$. This empirically confirms that the engine remains functional and avoids flagging even when remaining time is measured in fractions of a second.
