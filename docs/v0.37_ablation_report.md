## Executive Summary

Project Janus milestone v0.37 was commissioned to execute a controlled three-arm time-management ablation study under $3000\text{ ms} + 100\text{ ms}$ Fischer increment time controls. The primary research objective was to isolate the causal contribution of incorporating the Fischer increment into move-time allocation ($W_{\text{inc}} = 0.85$ vs $W_{\text{inc}} = 0.00$), holding the safety reserve ($50\text{ ms}$) and default moves-to-go estimate ($30$) strictly constant.

### Key Scientific Findings

1. **Increment Weight is the Primary Driver of Strength Improvement (Primary Comparison: Arm B vs Arm C):**
   When the safety reserve ($50\text{ ms}$) and moves-to-go estimate ($30$) are held identical, increasing the increment weight from $0.00$ to $0.85$ produces a massive, statistically significant playing-strength improvement:
   - **Score:** **70.5%** ($64\text{ wins}, 13\text{ draws}, 23\text{ losses}$) across 100 valid games (50 opening pairs).
   - **Elo Difference:** **+151.3 Elo** ($95\%\text{ CI: } [+80.2, +237.1]$).
   - **Paired Mean Differential:** $\bar{\Delta} = +0.2050$ ($SE = 0.04556$).
   - **Student's $t$-test ($df=49$):** $t = 4.4992$, $p = 4.20 \times 10^{-5}$ (Holm-adjusted $p = 8.41 \times 10^{-5}$).
   - **10,000-Resample Pair Bootstrap 95% CI:** Score $[0.6150, 0.7900]$, Elo $[+81.4, +230.2]$.
   - **Decisive Pairs Sign Test:** $29\text{ won}, 13\text{ tied}, 8\text{ lost}$ ($p = 7.53 \times 10^{-4}$).

2. **Reserve and Moves-to-Go Changes Without Increment Weight Produce No Strength Gain (Secondary Comparison: Arm A vs Arm B):**
   Comparing the original reference baseline ($R=0\text{ ms}, M=40, W_{\text{inc}}=0.00$) against the matched reference ($R=50\text{ ms}, M=30, W_{\text{inc}}=0.00$) demonstrates parity:
   - **Score:** **48.5%** ($38\text{ wins}, 21\text{ draws}, 41\text{ losses}$) across 100 valid games.
   - **Elo Difference:** **-10.4 Elo** ($95\%\text{ CI: } [-68.1, +46.6]$).
   - **Paired Mean Differential:** $\bar{\Delta} = -0.0150$ ($SE = 0.04066$, $t = -0.3689$, $p = 0.7138$).
   - **Conclusion:** Altering reserve and horizon parameters without increment awareness does not improve playing strength.

3. **Combined Policy Advantage Replicated (Contextual Comparison: Arm A vs Arm C):**
   Arm C (Adaptive) vs Arm A (Original Reference) confirms and strengthens the v0.36 result:
   - **Score:** **74.0%** ($65\text{ wins}, 18\text{ draws}, 17\text{ losses}$), **+181.7 Elo**.
   - **Student's $t$-test ($df=49$):** $t = 6.2437$, $p = 9.85 \times 10^{-8}$ (Holm-adjusted $p = 2.95 \times 10^{-7}$).

4. **Multiple Comparison Correction (Holm-Bonferroni):**
   Across the family of 3 hypotheses, both the primary ($H_1$) and contextual ($H_3$) comparisons remain overwhelmingly statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.05$.

## 4. Time-Management and Search Diagnostics

Detailed telemetry was collected across **27,431 moves** in the three matches:

| Metric | Arm A (Orig Ref) | Arm B (Matched Ref) | Arm C (Adaptive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Moves Logged** | 9,410 | 9,082 | 8,939 |
| **Median Duration** | $94.0\text{ ms}$ | $109.0\text{ ms}$ | **$141.0\text{ ms}$** ($+50.0\%$) |
| **Mean Nodes / Move** | 872.7 | 1,004.6 | **1,418.1** ($+62.5\%$) |
| **Mean Completed Depth** | 1.86 plies | 1.90 plies | **2.00 plies** |
| **Median Remaining Clock** | $3,154.0\text{ ms}$ | $2,851.0\text{ ms}$ | **$1,421.0\text{ ms}$** |
| **Emergency Allocations** | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| **Time Forfeits / Flags** | **0** | **0** | **0** |

### The Causal Mechanism
- **Arm A & Arm B ($W_{\text{inc}} = 0.00$):** Both reference policies ignore the $100\text{ ms}$ increment when computing move budgets. Consequently, both policies accumulate surplus time, ending games with median clocks of $3,154\text{ ms}$ and $2,851\text{ ms}$ (greater than or near starting clocks). They hoard time while searching fewer nodes per move ($872$ and $1,004$ nodes).
- **Arm C ($W_{\text{inc}} = 0.85$):** Re-invests $85\%$ of each $100\text{ ms}$ increment into current move searches ($141\text{ ms}$ median duration), achieving $1,418$ nodes/move ($+62.5\%$ search volume). This additional tactical horizon directly drives the $+151.3$ Elo advantage.
- **Clock Safety:** Zero emergency allocations and zero flags occurred. Arm C operated safely with median clock $1,421\text{ ms}$, well above the $50\text{ ms}$ reserve.

## 5. Multiple-Comparison Analysis (Holm-Bonferroni)

Using a family-wise significance level of $\alpha = 0.05$ across the three hypotheses:

1. **Rank 1 ($H_3$: Arm A vs Arm C):** Raw $p = 9.85 \times 10^{-8} < \frac{0.05}{3} = 0.0167$. Adjusted $p = 2.95 \times 10^{-7}$. **Significant.**
2. **Rank 2 ($H_1$: Arm B vs Arm C):** Raw $p = 4.20 \times 10^{-5} < \frac{0.05}{2} = 0.0250$. Adjusted $p = 8.41 \times 10^{-5}$. **Significant.**
3. **Rank 3 ($H_2$: Arm A vs Arm B):** Raw $p = 0.7138 \not< \frac{0.05}{1} = 0.0500$. Adjusted $p = 0.7138$. **Not Significant.**

## 6. Scientific Conclusion

### Decision: **SUPPORTED**

The pre-registered primary analysis conclusively establishes that:
1. Increasing increment weight from $0.00$ to $0.85$ produces a statistically significant and substantial playing-strength improvement ($+151.3$ Elo, $p = 4.20 \times 10^{-5}$) when safety reserve and moves-to-go estimate are held constant.
2. The increment-weight parameter is the primary causal driver of the playing-strength advantage discovered in Project Janus.
3. The safety-reserve and horizon changes without increment awareness (Arm A vs Arm B) provide no statistically discernible strength advantage ($p = 0.7138$).
