## 4. Phase 3 — King-Safety K1 Statistical Audit & TOST Equivalence

### 4.1 Resolution of the Equivalence Fallacy
The v0.40 report stated that Candidate K1 (disabling uncastled king safety) was *"statistically equivalent to baseline (p = 0.9231)"*. In Null Hypothesis Significance Testing (NHST):

$$\text{Failing to reject } H_0 \ne \text{Proof that } H_0 \text{ is true}$$

With $N=25$ pairs, the 95% bootstrap confidence interval on the paired score delta spans $[-19.0\%, +21.0\%]$ ($\approx \pm 140\text{ Elo}$). Claiming equivalence when the uncertainty interval spans nearly 300 Elo is a textbook statistical fallacy.

### 4.2 Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) Framework
We formalized the statistical evaluation under a formal equivalence testing framework. Defining an equivalence bound $\Delta_{\text{eq}} = \pm 10.0\%$ ($\Delta_{\text{pts}} = \pm 0.20$ points per pair, $\approx \pm 70\text{ Elo}$):

- **Null Hypothesis 1 ($H_{01}$):** $\bar{\delta} \le -0.20$ (K1 is unacceptably worse than K0)
  $$t_1 = \frac{\bar{\delta} - (-\Delta_{\text{eq}})}{SE} = \frac{0.0200 - (-0.20)}{0.2071} = +1.0622 \quad (p_1 \approx 0.150)$$
- **Null Hypothesis 2 ($H_{02}$):** $\bar{\delta} \ge +0.20$ (K1 is unacceptably better than K0)
  $$t_2 = \frac{\bar{\delta} - \Delta_{\text{eq}}}{SE} = \frac{0.0200 - 0.20}{0.2071} = -0.8690 \quad (p_2 \approx 0.197)$$

Because both $p_1 > 0.05$ and $p_2 > 0.05$, **neither null hypothesis is rejected**. The statistical evidence is underpowered and cannot support an equivalence claim.

### Corrected Verdict
The empirical data shows **"statistically inconclusive / no detectable difference at $N=50$"**. Because K0 includes chess-theoretically sound uncastled king safety penalties, Baseline K0 is retained as the production default (Full report: [`analysis/v0.41/k1_statistical_audit.md`](file:///e:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.41/k1_statistical_audit.md)).

## 5. Phase 4 — Threefold Repetition & Quiescence Correctness

### 5.1 Call-Path Diagnostic
In v0.40, `can_claim_threefold_repetition()` was removed from quiescence search to recover 47.9% NPS overhead, backed by the Capture Quiescence Irreversibility Theorem. However, an architectural gap remained:
1. When a position was reached that had occurred 2 times in preceding game history, and root search pushed a candidate move that reached the 3rd repetition at $d=0$, search entered `_quiescence`.
2. Inside `_quiescence`, the engine checked `is_insufficient_material()`, `is_fivefold_repetition()`, and `is_seventyfive_moves()`, but **did not check threefold repetition**.
3. Consequently, if the side to move was down heavy material (e.g. -1400 cp down Queen + Rook), quiescence evaluated `stand_pat = -1400`, completely failing to recognize the repetition draw score (0).

### 5.2 Sub-Microsecond Remedy
Benchmarking revealed that `board.can_claim_threefold_repetition()` was slow ($256.11\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) because it generated and simulated all legal moves. In contrast, `board.is_repetition(3)` performs a direct occupancy check on `board._transposition_key()` in **$0.18\text{ }\mu\text{s}$** ($1,438\times$ faster).

We applied `(ply > 0 and board.is_repetition(3))` at:
- `_negamax` (line 733)
- `_quiescence` (line 637)
- `_terminal_score` (line 508)

Restricting to `ply > 0` ensures root move generation is not aborted while immediately detecting repetition draws at child plies.

### 5.3 Empirical Verification
1. Down material, engine now immediately recognizes repetition moves:
   - Before patch: `Best move: e8f8, Score: -1400`
   - After patch: `Best move: e8d8, Score: 0` (Claims draw)
2. Ahead in material, engine correctly avoids repetition draws when winning alternatives exist (`score > 500`).
3. 7 unit tests authoritatively validate all repetition and quiescence invariants in [`tests/test_v041_repetition_quiescence.py`](file:///e:/ChessXML/project-janus/tests/test_v041_repetition_quiescence.py) (Full report: [`analysis/v0.41/repetition_and_quiescence_report.md`](file:///e:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.41/repetition_and_quiescence_report.md)).
