## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.46 established a rigorous, reproducible baseline of search efficiency, move-ordering effectiveness, and decision quality across the engine's 12-stage search pipeline. In strict adherence to milestone governance, no speculative pruning heuristics or production search mutations were implemented.

Through zero-overhead move-ordering diagnostic instrumentation (`--diag-ordering`) and a 37-position benchmark corpus evaluated against Stockfish 19 at depths 8–14:
1. **$75.79\%$ of all beta cutoffs occur on the first searched move** (Index 0).
2. **Eager full move-list sorting** (`moves.sort()` on 35+ moves) creates substantial computational waste at cutoff nodes, consuming **$14.305$ seconds** at depth 5 (~$7.4\%$ of total engine search runtime).
3. **Quiet move ordering degrades sharply** in non-tactical structures, dropping first-move cutoff rates to **$53.97\%\text{--}58.86\%$** when killer moves fail.
4. **Decision quality plateaus at depth 4–5** (best-move agreement with Stockfish 14 at $40.5\%$), limited by evaluation granularity rather than search depth alone.
5. **RFP yields a $9.19\%\text{--}10.60\%$ node reduction** while maintaining **$100\%$ move agreement with K0** across all 148 position-depth trials ($37 \times 4$).

## 3. Prioritized Bottlenecks & Optimization Road Map

1. **Staged / Lazy Move Selection (`MovePicker`):**
   Replace eager full-list sorting with a staged generator yielding TT move $\to$ MVV-LVA captures $\to$ killers $\to$ quiet history. Estimated performance gain: $5\%\text{--}15\%$ search speedup with 0 move divergence.
2. **Decayed & Depth-Scaled History Heuristic:**
   Address quiet move ordering collapse ($53.9\%$ first-move cutoffs in positional structures) by adding gravity aging and depth-squared scoring.
3. **Budget Stability Time Management:**
   Prevent mid-iteration quantization waste under blitz controls without risking time-forfeit regressions.
