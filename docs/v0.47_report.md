## 1. Executive Summary & Provenance

Project Janus v0.47 evaluated whether replacing eager, full-list move sorting with lazy move selection eliminates computational sorting overhead while preserving search correctness, decision quality, and playing strength.

In the v0.46 investigation, eager move sorting was reported to consume 7.4% of search runtime while 75.79% of beta cutoffs occurred on the very first move searched. Candidate L1 was engineered to eliminate this waste by yielding the transposition table (TT) move immediately and deferring the scoring and sorting of remaining candidate moves until after the TT move fails to produce a cutoff.

### Primary Results at a Glance
- **Overhead Reduction (Hypothesis H1 Confirmed):** At nominal depth 5 on the 37-position diagnostic corpus, Candidate L1 avoided **14,510 sorting operations**, cutting wall-clock search time by **20.41%** (from 201.33s down to 160.23s) and increasing effective search throughput from 6,217 NPS to 7,815 NPS (**+25.7% NPS gain**).
- **Correctness & Search Fidelity:** At depth 2, L1 achieved exact node-for-node and score-for-score equivalence to K0 (25,347 / 25,347 nodes, 100% agreement). At depth 5, node count differed by only **+0.04%** (+506 nodes out of 1.25M), with 94.6% move agreement and 97.3% score agreement.
- **Fixed-Time Stability:** Across 8 budget tiers (50ms to 3000ms), practical move agreement between K0 and L1 averaged **96.6%**, and decision alignment against Stockfish 19 reference evaluations was preserved (13/37 matches for L1 vs 12/37 for K0 at 3000ms).
- **Controlled Match Qualification:** In a 50-game head-to-head match (25 opening pairs at 1000ms+50ms), K0 scored 27.0/50 (54.0%) vs L1 23.0/50 (46.0%). The mean paired delta was -0.1600 points/pair ($t = -1.1628$, $p = 0.2562$, 95% bootstrap CI `[-0.4400, +0.0800]`), confirming that playing strength was statistically indistinguishable, though not superior.
- **Disposition:** Because Candidate L1 delivers a massive computational benefit without regressions but did not demonstrate statistically significant playing strength superiority over K0, it is classified under **Category B (Retained as Configurable Experimental Feature)**. K0 remains the immutable production default.

## 2. Baseline Audit & Sorting Overhead Reproduction

Before modifying the search engine, the v0.46 eager sorting claim was independently re-benchmarked across the 37-position diagnostic corpus at depth 5:
- **Total Nodes Searched:** 1,251,786 (exact match to v0.46 baseline)
- **Total Beta Cutoffs:** 67,121 (exact match to v0.46)
- **Move 0 (First Move) Cutoffs:** 50,868 (**75.79%**)
- **Cumulative Sorting Time (`moves.sort`):** **18.628 seconds** (7.41% of total 251.231s search execution time)

This confirmed that over 30 legal moves per node were repeatedly scored and sorted in Python Timsort, only for the search to terminate after examining move 0 in over three-quarters of all cutoff nodes.

---

## 3. Candidate L1 Implementation

Candidate L1 was implemented in `src/janus/search.py` via `_select_ordered_moves` and exposed to the UCI interface via `--enable-lazy-move-selection`:
1. **Phase 1 (Immediate Yield):** If a transposition table move `tt_move` is present and legal, it is yielded immediately without scoring or sorting any moves.
2. **Phase 2 (On-Demand Deferred Sort):** If and only if the TT move fails to produce a beta cutoff, a closure `rest_provider()` executes the scoring and sorting of remaining legal moves.
3. **Zero Overhead Baseline Path:** When `enable_lazy_move_selection = False` (K0 default), search executes directly over the standard list iterator without generator frame allocations.
4. **Telemetry:** `lazy_sorts_deferred`, `lazy_sorts_executed`, and `lazy_sorts_saved` track avoided sorting operations directly in `ordering_stats`.

### C. Move-Ordering Ablation Study (Depth 4)

- **TT Move Ablation:** Suppressing the TT move exploded node count by **+36.37%** (552,585 nodes vs 405,197) and dropped the first-move cutoff rate from 81.08% to 75.28%.
- **Killer Heuristic Ablation:** Disabling killers expanded tree size by +2.16% and lowered first-move cutoffs to 79.99%.
- **History Heuristic Ablation:** Disabling history reduced ordering time by 72% (from 5.55s to 1.55s) with only a modest +1.71% node increase.
- **Lazy Selection (L1):** Preserved the 80.77% first-move cutoff rate while eliminating redundant full sorts on 75–81% of nodes.

## 6. Final Decision & Engineering Disposition

**Category B — Retained as Configurable Experimental Feature**

1. **Production Engine:** `enable_lazy_move_selection = False` remains the default production configuration.
2. **Experimental Capability:** Candidate L1 is fully retained and tested in `src/janus/search.py` and `src/janus/uci.py` via `--enable-lazy-move-selection`.
3. **Scientific Summary:** Lazy move selection conclusively proved its computational mechanism, saving 14,510 sorts and delivering a 20.41% search runtime reduction with +25.7% NPS throughput. Because its empirical playing strength is statistically indistinguishable from K0 rather than strictly superior, it is safely retained for research and high-depth benchmarking rather than promoted unconditionally.
