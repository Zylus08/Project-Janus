## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.48 conducted an exhaustive diagnostic and experimental investigation into the search-efficiency benefits, move-ordering mechanics, history-heuristic overheads, and search-divergence characteristics of Lazy Move Selection (Candidate L1) against the production reference (Baseline K0).

In v0.47, Candidate L1 demonstrated a ~20% fixed-depth search runtime reduction on the 37-position diagnostic corpus, but a 50-game paired match did not establish playing-strength superiority ($p = 0.256$). v0.48 resolved the underlying questions through controlled ablations, forensic divergence analysis, history-heuristic profiling, incremental move selection experiments (Candidate L2), and multi-tier fixed-time decision validations:

1. **Decomposition of L1 Speedup:** The wall-clock benefit of L1 stems overwhelmingly from **immediate TT-move beta cutoffs**. At Depth 5, L1 avoided **14,510 complete move-list sorts** out of 67,789 total cutoffs. 21.42% of all cutoffs (28.18% of first-move cutoffs) occurred on the immediate TT move before any other moves were generated, scored, or sorted.
2. **The History Heuristic Bottleneck (99.1% Waste Ratio):** At Depth 4, eager move sorting executed **975,344 quiet move scoring and history table lookups**, yet only **8,326 quiet moves actually produced beta cutoffs** (a 117:1 waste ratio, or 99.1% wasted computation). Candidate L1 achieves speedup precisely because the TT-first path bypasses this massive computational waste whenever the TT move cuts off.
3. **Forensic Safety of Search Divergences:** Across all 148 paired search outputs across Depths 2–5, **142/148 (95.95%)** produced exact move-and-score identity. Exactly 4 moves differed (2.7%), all classified as `alternate_equal_line` ($\le 3$ cp difference between quiet development moves). There were **0 blunders, 0 tactical regressions, and 0 material drops**. In position `control_p7_white_ply3` at Depth 5, L1 selected the Stockfish 19 reference move `d2d4`, which K0 missed.
4. **Stable Incremental Selection (Candidate L2):** Implementing stable entry-scored incremental selection achieved **exact 100.0% node-for-node and score-for-score equivalence to K0** (405,197 nodes at Depth 4). However, in interpreted Python, pre-scoring and maintaining move wrappers incurred a $+16.9\%$ runtime penalty ($-14.5\%$ NPS regression), confirming that Candidate L1's two-phase generator is the only viable architecture in Python.
5. **Fixed-Time Decision Quality:** Across 7 budget tiers (50ms to 3000ms), L1 matched K0's moves with 100.0% identity at short time controls (50–180ms) and maintained equivalent Stockfish 19 match rates across all tiers (e.g. 27.0% vs 27.0% at 180ms; 32.4% vs 32.4% at 500ms).
6. **Milestone Disposition:** Candidate L1 is retained under **Category B (Efficiency Improvement Retained, Strength Unconfirmed)**. K0 remains the immutable production default (`enable_lazy_move_selection=False`, `enable_rfp=False`).

## 4. Controlled TT-Path Ablation (Phase C)

To isolate the exact algorithmic contribution of yielding the TT move first versus lazy deferred sorting, Phase C evaluated 5 distinct configurations at Depth 4:
1. `K0_Cold`: Eager sort, unseeded transposition table.
2. `L1_Cold`: Lazy TT-first selection, unseeded transposition table.
3. `T0_EagerSort_TTFirst`: Eager sort performed immediately, but TT move yielded first.
4. `K0_Warm`: Eager sort with TT pre-seeded by shallow search (Depth 2).
5. `L1_Warm`: Lazy TT-first selection with TT pre-seeded by shallow search (Depth 2).

### Comparative Ablation Results (Depth 4):

| Configuration | Nodes | Ordering Time | Wall-Clock Time | Move 0 Cutoffs | First-Move Cutoff % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **K0_Cold** | 405,197 | 2.50 s | 32.48 s | 16,630 | 76.28% |
| **T0_EagerSort_TTFirst** | **405,197** | 2.48 s | 32.12 s | 16,630 | 76.28% |
| **L1_Cold** | 407,705 | **1.83 s** | **27.67 s** | 16,626 | 76.26% |
| **K0_Warm** | 228,057 | 1.42 s | 18.55 s | 15,310 | 83.42% |
| **L1_Warm** | 228,810 | **1.05 s** | **15.82 s** | 15,308 | 83.40% |

### Ablation Takeaways:
- **T0 Equivalence:** Yielding the TT move first within an eager sort framework produces the exact same search tree as K0 (405,197 nodes), proving that TT-first ordering is semantically sound.
- **Lazy vs Eager:** Comparing T0 (32.12s) to L1_Cold (27.67s) reveals that the **$-13.9\%$ speedup** between them is solely attributable to skipping the eager sort when the TT move cuts off.
- **TT Warmth Amplification:** In a warm TT environment (typical of iterative deepening), tree size shrinks by 43.7% and first-move cutoffs rise to 83.4%, where L1 cuts search time from 18.55s down to 15.82s ($-14.7\%$).
