## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.54 investigated whether native **killer-move** and **history-heuristic** move ordering can improve search efficiency, tactical decision quality, and playing strength in the compiled C++17 Janus search engine under equal Fischer time controls, without compromising alpha-beta correctness or legal move generation.

### Key Milestones Achieved:

Audited toolchain (MSVC v19.43.34809 x64, Python 3.13.2 64-bit, Stockfish 19 verified). Confirmed that previous native engines (N2, N3, N4) assigned an unranked score of 0 to all quiet moves (`return 0; // Quiet moves`).

   - **Killer Heuristic:** Formulated 2 killer slots per search ply (`g_killer_moves[64][2]`) restricted strictly to non-capture, non-promotion beta cutoffs. Implemented primary-to-secondary slot demotion and strict duplicate suppression.
   - **History Heuristic:** Formulated a 32 KB global history table (`g_history_table[2][64][64]`) tracking moving side, from-square, and to-square. Scaled cutoff rewards quadratically by search depth ($\text{bonus} = \text{depth}^2$), capped at 70,000 (strictly below Killer 2 at 80,000). Applied generational aging (decay by 2) at the root of each search invocation.

### 2.2 Native Codebase Reconnaissance
Prior to v0.54, the compiled C++17 search cores (v0.50 N2, v0.52 N3, v0.53 N4) implemented move scoring via `score_move_for_ordering()`:
- Transposition table best moves were scored at `10,000,000`.
- Pawn promotions were scored at `800,000` to `900,000`.
- Captures were scored using MVV-LVA (`100,000 + 10 * victim - attacker`).
- **All quiet moves were assigned 0**:
  ```cpp
  // Lines 1261-1262 of v0.53 janus_native.cpp
  return 0; // Quiet moves
  ```
Consequently, quiet moves were searched in arbitrary pseudo-legal generation order (order of generation from square 0 to 63). In positions where quiet moves refuted an opponent's line, the search was forced to traverse dozens of non-refuting quiet moves before stumbling upon the cutoff move.

## 3. Move Ordering Architecture Audit (Phase 1)

The complete search move selection architecture was audited to guarantee that adding killer and history ordering would never displace tactical moves (captures and promotions) or the transposition table move.

### The 7-Tier Strict Score Separation
```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 1: Transposition Table Best Move (PV Move)        Score: 10,000,000 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 2: Pawn Promotions (Queen: 900,000, Rook: 800,000)                  |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 3: Tactical Captures via MVV-LVA             Score: 100,100–100,505 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 4: Primary Killer Move (Killer 1)                 Score:     90,000 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 5: Secondary Killer Move (Killer 2)               Score:     80,000 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 6: History Heuristic Dynamic Score                Score:   1–70,000 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 7: Unscored Quiet Moves                           Score:          0 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Mathematical Separation Proof:
1. Minimum Capture Score: Queen capturing Pawn $= 100,000 + 10 \times 100 - 900 = 100,100$.
2. Killer 1 Score: $90,000$. Gap to minimum capture $= 10,100 > 0$.
3. Killer 2 Score: $80,000$. Gap to Killer $1 = 10,000 > 0$.
4. Maximum History Score: Hard ceiling at $70,000$. Gap to Killer $2 = 10,000 > 0$.
5. Minimum History Score: $1$. Gap to unscored quiet $= 1 > 0$.

This guarantees that:
- Captures always precede quiet killers and history moves.
- Killer 1 strictly precedes Killer 2.
- Both killer moves strictly precede all history moves.
- Any history move with positive credit precedes unproven quiet moves.

## 4. Formal Heuristic Specifications (Phases 2 & 3)

### 4.1 Killer Heuristic Specification
- **Storage:** `uint16_t g_killer_moves[64][2]` (256 bytes, fits completely in L1 cache).
- **Eligibility:** Move $m$ must cause a beta cutoff ($\text{score} \ge \beta$), must **not** be a capture (`m.captured_piece == 0`), and must **not** be a pawn promotion.
- **Update Logic:**
  - If $m == \text{killer}[ply][0]$, no update is performed (prevents duplicate pollution).
  - If $m \ne \text{killer}[ply][0]$, primary slot demotes to secondary slot:
    $$\text{killer}[ply][1] \leftarrow \text{killer}[ply][0]$$
    $$\text{killer}[ply][0] \leftarrow m$$
- **Clearing:** Completely zeroed at the start of every search invocation (`native_search()`).

### 4.2 History Heuristic Specification
- **Storage:** `int g_history_table[2][64][64]` (8,192 32-bit integers $= 32\text{ KB}$, fits comfortably in CPU L1/L2 cache).
- **Indexing:** Indexed by `[side_to_move][from_square][to_square]`.
- **Eligibility:** Move $m$ must cause a beta cutoff ($\text{score} \ge \beta$), must not be a capture, and must not be a promotion.
- **Increment Policy:**
  $$\text{bonus} = \text{depth}^2$$
  where $\text{depth}$ is the remaining search depth from the current node. This quadratically rewards quiet moves that cut off deep subtrees.
- **Saturation Ceiling:** Clamped at 70,000:
  $$\text{history}[side][from][to] \leftarrow \min(70000, \text{history}[side][from][to] + \text{depth}^2)$$
- **Generational Aging:** To prevent stale history from prior moves from distorting current root positions, all history entries are decayed by 2 at the beginning of each search:
  $$\text{history}[s][f][t] \leftarrow \lfloor \text{history}[s][f][t] / 2 \rfloor$$

   - History scores provide global positional guidance across all plies.
   - When combined ($C_3$), Killer moves score 87,187 cutoffs and History scores 91,643 cutoffs, together resolving **$53.9\%$ of all cutoffs** in the tree and driving the first-move cutoff rate to $83.23\%$.
