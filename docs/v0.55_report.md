## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.55 investigated two distinct research questions:
1. **Primary Research Question:** Does improved killer/history move ordering make Principal Variation Search (PVS) more effective, converting the search tree savings observed in v0.53 into a statistically demonstrable playing-strength improvement?
2. **Secondary Research Question:** Does the $+29.6$ Elo advantage reported for N5 over N3 in v0.54 replicate on a genuinely independent, previously unseen opening corpus?

### 1.1 Headline Findings

#### Primary PVS Promotion Match (Experiment A: N6 vs N5):
- **Result:** In a preregistered 200-game match under equal Fischer 3.0s + 100ms time controls using 100 fresh master opening pairs, Candidate N6 scored **99.0 / 200 (49.50%, +40 =118 -42)** against Baseline N5.
- **Estimated Elo Advantage:** **$-3.47\text{ Elo}$** [95% Bootstrap CI: **$[-34.86, +29.60]\text{ Elo}$**].
- **Paired Student’s t-Test:** $t = -0.2098, df = 99, \mathbf{p = 0.83425}$ (statistically indistinguishable from zero).
- **Outcome:** PVS achieves measurable search tree compression ($-4.25\%$ nodes at Depth 6, $+0.5$ plies median depth at 3000ms), but this computational saving **does not translate into playing strength**. Category A promotion is denied; Janus Native N5 is retained as production baseline.

#### Independent Strength Replication Match (Experiment B: N5 vs N3):
- **Result:** In an independent 200-game match under identical conditions using the fresh 100-pair opening corpus, Candidate N5 defeated Baseline N3 decisively: **122.0 / 200 (61.00%, +68 =108 -24)**.
- **Net Score Advantage:** $+44.0$ points ($+22.00\%$).
- **Estimated Elo Advantage:** $\mathbf{+77.71\text{ Elo}}$ [95% Bootstrap CI: $\mathbf{[+45.42, +111.37]\text{ Elo}}$].
- **Paired Student’s t-Test:** $t = 4.7586, df = 99, \mathbf{p = 0.000007 < 0.0001}$.
- **Outcome:** The playing strength advantage of native killer and history move ordering replicates overwhelmingly out-of-sample, conclusively resolving the statistical fragility observed in v0.54.

## 11. Answers to Governing Research Questions (Phase 11)

1. **Did improved ordering change PVS's null-window effectiveness?** Yes. Move ordering raises first-move cutoffs to $>83\%$, drastically shrinking the unpruned non-PV tail where PVS operates.
2. **Did PVS improve N5's fixed-depth efficiency?** Yes. PVS pruned an additional $-4.25\%$ nodes at Depth 6 (5,336,701 vs 5,573,753 nodes).
3. **Did PVS improve N5's fixed-time decisions?** Marginally. PVS unlocked $+0.5$ plies of median depth at 3000ms, but move choices remained $87.5\%$ to $100\%$ identical.
4. **Did PVS improve N5's playing strength?** No. Under equal Fischer 3.0s + 100ms time controls, Candidate N6 scored $49.50\%$ ($-3.47$ Elo, $p = 0.834$).
5. **Did N5's historical strength gain replicate?** Decisively. On 100 previously unseen opening pairs, N5 defeated N3 $122.0 / 200$ ($61.00\%$, $+77.71$ Elo, $p = 0.000007$).
6. **Were results consistent across opening structures?** Yes. In Experiment B, N5 achieved superior scores across all 5 ECO categories.
7. **Were results sensitive to color or adjudication?** No. Outcomes were symmetrical across White ($63.0\%$) and Black ($59.0\%$), with zero safety-limit hits.
8. **What mechanisms explain the observed interaction?** Sub-additive tree overlap: killer and history moves prune the search tree prior to null-window exploration.
9. **What limitations remain due to sample size and time control?** A $+5$ to $+10$ Elo effect size would require $>2,000$ games to detect at $\alpha=0.05$. At $3.0\text{s}+0.1\text{s}$, a $4.25\%$ speedup is below the threshold needed to gain a full iteration in most middlegame positions.
10. **What evidence is still needed before broader strength claims?** Longer time controls (e.g. 10.0s + 200ms) where iterative deepening increments are larger, and evaluation enhancements that provide greater non-PV discrimination.
