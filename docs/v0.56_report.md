## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.56 developed, mathematically verified, and scientifically evaluated a native C++17 Static Exchange Evaluation (SEE) capture ordering subsystem in the Janus bitboard search core. The investigation was designed to determine whether resolving MVV-LVA's structural ordering flaw—scoring suicidal captures ahead of quiet killer moves—improves alpha-beta search efficiency and playing strength under Fischer blitz controls, strictly isolating capture ordering from capture pruning.

### Primary Milestone Outcomes:
1. **Mathematical & Architectural Correctness:** Implemented a full recursive least-valuable-attacker bitboard swap algorithm accounting for dynamic x-ray battery unmasking, en passant captures, promotions, and king-defense legality. Achieved **25/25 (100%) passing tests** against an exhaustive legal-minimax oracle with zero discrepancies.
2. **Search Semantics & Bit-Exact Parity:** Proved bit-exact parity between the newly compiled engine with SEE disabled (`S0`) and the audited v0.55 production baseline (`N5`): identical node counts, evaluations, and PV moves across all depths.
3. **Fixed-Depth Search Tree Compression:** In a 69-position factorial ablation across Depths 3 to 6:
   - Search trees were compressed by **-14.75%** at Depth 4, **-7.82%** at Depth 5, and **-7.35%** at Depth 6 (eliminating **517,176 nodes** at Depth 6).
   - First-move beta-cutoff frequency jumped from **84.5% to 96.3%** (+11.8 percentage points).
   - Net search speedup of **+6.79%** in wall-clock time was achieved despite evaluating up to 2.4 million exchanges.
4. **Tactical Safety Invariance:** Across 64 positions in 4 tactical benchmark suites, Candidate S3 maintained **16/16 solved solutions (100% solution retention, 0 dropped)**, agreed with Baseline S0 on **64/64 (100.0%) of move selections**, and solved the suites with **-9.05% fewer nodes**.
5. **Fixed-Time Search Dynamics:** Evaluated across 7 time tiers (50ms to 3000ms), SEE capture ordering sustained elevated first-move cutoffs (+3.5% to +4.0%), zero clock overshoot, and enabled Candidate S3 to search an extra iterative deepening ply in tactical scenarios (Depth 8 vs Depth 7 in `val_14_tactical_pin`).
6. **Preregistered Match Result & Governance Disposition:** In a preregistered 200-game match over 100 fresh, disjoint master opening pairs under Fischer 3.0s+100ms time controls:
   - Candidate N7 scored **95.5 / 200 (47.75%)** [+33 wins, =125 draws, -42 losses].
   - Paired Student's t-test: $t = -1.0844$ ($df = 99$, $p = 0.2808$).
   - Performance Elo: **$-15.65$ Elo** [95% bootstrap CI: $-43.66$ to $+12.17$ Elo].
   - Because $p > 0.05$ and the confidence interval spans zero, the result is statistically neutral.
   - Per preregistered governance rules, Candidate N7 is assigned **Category B (Neutral Retention)**. The production baseline remains **Janus Native N5**, while the verified C++17 SEE subsystem is permanently retained behind UCI configuration options (`UseSEEOrdering`, `UseSEEInQuiescence`) as the foundation for future SEE-based pruning research.

## 3. Capture Ordering Architecture Audit & Problem Formulation

In Janus Native N5, captures were scored solely via Most Valuable Victim - Least Valuable Attacker (MVV-LVA):
$$\text{Score} = 100,000 + (\text{Victim} \times 10) - \text{Attacker}$$
This heuristic suffers from a fatal architectural flaw:
- A defended pawn captured by a Queen ($Q \times P$) is scored at $100,000 + 10 - 5 = 100,005$.
- Proven quiet killer moves are scored at $90,000$ (Killer 1) and $80,000$ (Killer 2).
- Strong quiet history moves are scored up to $16,384$.
- As a consequence, the engine was forced to search suicidal captures ($Q \times P$, blunder of $-800$ cp) *before* examining quiet refutation killer moves that would immediately produce a beta cutoff.
- Full architectural audit is documented in [`analysis/v0.56/CAPTURE_ORDERING_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.56/CAPTURE_ORDERING_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md).

## 4. Formal SEE Specification & Bitboard Implementation

To rectify this flaw without pruning any legal moves, a native Static Exchange Evaluation (SEE) algorithm was specified and implemented in C++17 bitboards:

### 4.1 Recursive Bitboard Minimax Model
SEE computes the static material balance on target square $T$ by simulating a sequence of captures ordered strictly by Least Valuable Attacker ($P \prec N \prec B \prec R \prec Q \prec K$):
1. **Initial Gain:** $d_0 = \text{Value}(V)$, where $V$ is the victim on square $T$.
2. **Gain Recurrence:**
   $$d_k = \text{Value}(A_{k-1}) - \max(0, d_{k+1})$$
3. **Dynamic Battery Unmasking:** After each attacker $A_k$ moves, the target square occupancy bitboard is updated (`occupied ^= from_sq`), unmasking sliding x-ray attackers (rooks along files, bishops along diagonals, queens along both).
4. **Pin & Legality Mask:** Attackers pinned to the king along an angle non-collinear with the target square are disqualified from the exchange.
5. **Special Rules:** En passant captures credit $100$ cp for the captured pawn from its adjacent rank; pawn promotions credit the promoted piece value.

Full mathematical specification is recorded in [`analysis/v0.56/SEE_FORMAL_SPECIFICATION.md`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.56/SEE_FORMAL_SPECIFICATION.md).

## 5. Controlled Integration & 5-Tier Ordering Hierarchy

The SEE subsystem was integrated into `analysis/v0.56/native/janus_native.cpp` under the strict constraint that **zero moves are pruned**. Every legal capture remains searched.

### Main Search Scoring Hierarchy:
| Tier | Move Classification | Score Formula | Score Range |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **1** | Winning Captures ($\text{SEE} > 0$) | $100,000 + \text{MVV-LVA}$ | $100,000 - 100,050$ |
| **2** | Equal Trades ($\text{SEE} == 0$) | $95,000 + \text{MVV-LVA}$ | $95,000 - 95,050$ |
| **3** | **Primary Killer Move** | Fixed Constant | **$90,000$** |
| **4** | **Secondary Killer Move** | Fixed Constant | **$80,000$** |
| **5** | Losing Captures ($\text{SEE} < 0$) | $20,000 + \text{SEE} + \text{MVV-LVA}$ | $10,000 - 20,000$ |
| **6** | Butterfly History Quiets | History Table Entry | $0 - 16,384$ |
| **7** | Unscored Quiet Moves | Base Constant | $0$ |

### Quiescence Search Ordering:
Winning captures ($\text{SEE} > 0$) and equal trades ($\text{SEE} == 0$) are placed first, followed by losing captures ($\text{SEE} < 0$). In accordance with the non-pruning governing constraint, all captures remain eligible for search.

Full integration specification is recorded in [`analysis/v0.56/SEE_INTEGRATION_SPECIFICATION.md`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.56/SEE_INTEGRATION_SPECIFICATION.md).
