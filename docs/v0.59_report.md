## 1. Executive Summary

Project Janus v0.59 was commissioned to rigorously investigate why the v0.58 neural policy prototype produced a neutral tournament result ($+1.7$ Elo, $p = 0.9020$), establish whether learned policy information causally improves alpha-beta search efficiency, and determine the future trajectory of neural research in Project Janus.

Through exhaustive forensic audits, instrumented root-telemetry logging, four-way causal ablations, bounded gating studies, and held-out evaluation across 100 benchmark positions, this investigation delivered decisive mathematical and empirical findings:

1. **Phase 0 Forensic Audit:** Resolved both documented textual anomalies from v0.58. The model parameter count is confirmed at **652,488 parameters** across PyTorch checkpoint, C++ serialization header, on-disk binary, and runtime memory layout ($|\Delta| = 0.0$ bit-exact); the reported figure of 643,200 was a clerical calculation typo in the v0.58 markdown prose. The tournament scoreboard is confirmed at **36 wins, 129 draws, 35 losses** ($100.5 / 200$, $50.25\%$, $t = +0.1198$, $p = 0.9020$); the executive summary had cited the 126 threefold repetition draws, omitting the 3 dead-position material draws ($126 + 3 = 129$). Complete partition isolation ($0.00\%$ cross-split leakage) was verified across all 80,000 dataset positions.
2. **Phase 1 Root Override Discovery:** At iterative deepening depths $\ge 4$, the neural policy's top recommendation is overridden **$80.0\% - 86.0\%$ of the time**. In **$100.0\%$ of these override cases**, the overriding move is the transposition table (`tt_move`) preserved from iteration $d-1$ ($10,000,000$ score tier). In the $86\%$ of positions where classical ordering and neural policy disagree, classical ordering finds the eventual alpha-beta PV move **16 times more frequently** than the neural policy ($74.4\%$ vs $4.7\%$).
3. **Phase 2 Causal Invariance Proof:** In a 4-way causal ablation across 50 positions at depths 4, 5, and 6, **Condition C1 (Trained Policy), Condition C2 (Random Policy), and Condition C3 (Shuffled Logits) produced bit-exact, node-for-node identical search results** ($\Delta = 0.0\text{ nodes}, t = 0.000, p = 1.0000$). Because all quiet root moves fail low against the TT move, every quiet branch must be fully searched in an all-node tree, rendering total searched nodes mathematically invariant to quiet move permutation at the root. Learned chess knowledge contributes **zero** measurable efficiency to root move ordering.
4. **Phase 3 & 4 Gating and Held-Out Evaluation:** Extending policy ordering to internal nodes (G2) or along the principal variation (G3) under bounded latency caps ($0.5\%, 1.0\%, 2.0\%$) does not reduce tree size ($+1.20\%$ nodes at Depth 6) and does not increase nominal depth in fixed-time search ($6.34$ plies in C1 vs $6.32 - 6.38$ plies in candidates at 100ms).
5. **Phase 5 Candidate Selection Gate:** All candidate engines failed Criterion 5 (no search efficiency advantage) and Criterion 6 (zero superiority over randomized controls). In accordance with preregistered protocol, the Phase 6 tournament was **REFUSED AUTHORIZATION** and recorded as **NOT RUN**.

## 4. Phase 2 — Four-Way Causal Policy Control Experiments

To isolate the causal origin of search behavior, four policy conditions were evaluated across 50 positions at depths 4, 5, and 6:
- **C0:** Classical C1 Baseline (Frozen control).
- **C1:** Trained MicroPolicyNet (Learned chess knowledge).
- **C2:** Random Policy Ordering (Deterministic PRNG `splitmix64`, seed=42).
- **C3:** Shuffled Neural Logits (True output distribution, scrambled move mapping, seed=42).

### 4.1 Comparative Search Results
| Condition | Depth 4 Total Nodes | Depth 5 Total Nodes | Depth 6 Total Nodes | Move Agreement with C0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 4.2 Paired Hypothesis Testing ($\text{df}=49$)
$$\text{Nodes}(\text{C1: Trained}) - \text{Nodes}(\text{C2: Random}) = \mathbf{0.0\text{ nodes}} \quad (t = +0.000, \ p = 1.0000)$$
$$\text{Nodes}(\text{C1: Trained}) - \text{Nodes}(\text{C3: Shuffled}) = \mathbf{0.0\text{ nodes}} \quad (t = +0.000, \ p = 1.0000)$$

### 4.3 The Invariance Theorem
Because the TT move is searched first and establishes $\alpha$, all subsequent quiet root moves fail low ($\text{score} \le \alpha$). In an all-node tree where all moves must be searched to nominal depth $d-1$ to prove fail-low, the sum of independent subtrees is mathematically invariant to the permutation order of quiet moves:
$$\text{TotalNodes} = 1 + \text{Tree}(m_{\text{TT}}) + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \text{Tree}(m_{\text{quiet}, i})$$
Learned chess knowledge contributes exactly **0.0%** to search reduction. Any slight difference between C1 and C0 is 100% attributable to arbitrary quiet move permutation at the root.

## 7. Phase 7 — Research Decision & Classification

### 7.1 Final Classification
$$\mathbf{CATEGORY\ C\ —\ NEURAL\ POLICY\ INEFFECTIVE}$$
**Definition:** Evidence indicates that standalone neural policy move ordering within a classical alpha-beta framework is uncompetitive on CPU and provides zero measurable efficiency benefit over randomized controls.

### 7.2 Mandatory Next Direction
$$\mathbf{DIRECTION\ V\ —\ LEARNED\ VALUE\ EVALUATION\ (NNUE)}$$

### 7.3 Scientific Rationale for Direction V
1. **Minimax Is Evaluation-Driven:** Alpha-beta search prunes subtrees based on static evaluation bounds. A policy network only predicts human move popularity ($\pi(m)$), not minimax truth ($V(s)$).
2. **The Modern Computer Chess Paradigm:** The breakthrough in modern chess engines (Stockfish NNUE, Ethereal) was replacing handcrafted static evaluation functions with quantized, SIMD-accelerated neural value networks (evaluating in $\approx 20 - 50\ \text{ns}$), while retaining classical move ordering (TT, MVV-LVA, killers, history) which executes in nanoseconds.
3. **Pivoting to v0.60:** Direction V addresses the core deficiency of classical Janus (static positional evaluation) without slowing down the move-ordering pipeline.
