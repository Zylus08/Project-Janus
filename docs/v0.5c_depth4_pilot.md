## 1. Runtime Anomaly Investigation & Evidence

### The Observed Anomaly
- **Initial 2-Game Pilot (Depth 3):** 34.2 seconds total, averaging **17.1 seconds / game** (0.171 seconds / ply over 200 plies).
- **Completed 50-Game Baseline (Depth 3):** 4,921.9 seconds total, averaging **98.4 seconds / game** (1.069 seconds / ply over 4,606 plies).
- **Discrepancy:** The 50-game baseline was **~5.8x slower per game** and **~6.2x slower per ply** than the initial pilot.

### Empirical Investigation & Root Causes
Through diagnostic profiling on individual board states and code inspection of `scripts/match.py` and `src/janus/uci.py`, the following facts were established:

1. **Opening & Game Phase Selection Bias in the 2-Game Pilot:**
   - The initial pilot played Pair 1 (`scotch_game`). Game 1 transitioned rapidly into an endgame with very few pieces on the board and lasted 155 plies.
   - Diagnostic profiling of `JanusSearch.search(max_depth=3)` revealed:
     - **Endgame position (K+R vs K):** 692 nodes evaluated, taking **0.0358 seconds**.
     - **Complex middlegame position (King's Indian / Sicilian):** 10,200–11,500 nodes evaluated, taking **1.309–1.331 seconds**.
   - Because the 2-game pilot spent ~130 plies in simplified endgame states where moves executed in ~35ms, the pilot's average was artificially skewed downward to 0.171s/ply.
2. **Middlegame Complexity across the 50-Game Baseline:**
   - The 50-game baseline sampled 25 diverse openings, including tactical lines (Sicilians, King's Indian, Frenches, Caro-Kanns, Dutch Defense).
   - In full middlegames with 35–45 legal moves per position, pure-Python alpha-beta search evaluates ~10,000–15,000 nodes, requiring **1.0–1.5 seconds per Janus move**.
   - In games lasting 120–240 plies through heavy middlegames, Janus alone required 60–120 seconds of pure computation time per game.
3. **Nominal Depth vs Compute Asymmetry:**
   - Both engines received identical nominal depth limits (`depth=3`).
   - For Stockfish 19 (compiled C++ with NNUE and multithreading capabilities), depth 3 was evaluated in **~0.002 seconds (2 milliseconds)** per move.
   - For Janus (pure-Python iterative deepening with hand-crafted PST and material evaluation), depth 3 required **~1.3 seconds** per move.
   - Total game duration was therefore 99.8% dominated by Janus's search time; Stockfish's execution time was negligible.
4. **Verification of Stockfish Baseline Options:**
   - Inspection of baseline artifact metadata (`janus-stockfish-20261013-132047.json`) confirmed:
     `stockfish_skill: null` | `stockfish_elo: null` | `stockfish_threads: 1` | `stockfish_hash_mb: 128`
   - **No skill handicap or UCI_Elo limit was active.** Stockfish operated at full tactical capability, constrained solely by `depth 3`, 1 thread, and 128 MB hash.

## 4. Depth-3 vs Depth-4 Observations & Decisions

### Tactical & Horizon Observations
1. **Horizon Expansion:**
   - At depth 3, Janus frequently fell victim to 3-ply horizon traps, while Stockfish occasionally overlooked tactics beyond ply 3 (e.g. blundering queens in Pair 7 of the baseline).
   - At depth 4, search horizons pushed tactical blunders past 4 plies. In Game 1 (`scotch_game`), Janus found tactical defenses `8. Nb5!` and navigated Queen checks to a secure threefold repetition draw.
   - In Game 2, Janus held a 76-ply defense as Black against Stockfish, forcing Stockfish into a perpetual check repetition.
2. **Runtime Scaling (Depth 3 vs Depth 4):**
   - **Average time per ply:** Increased from 1.069s (depth 3) to 3.921s (depth 4) — a **3.67x increase**.
   - **Average game duration:** Increased from 98.4s (depth 3) to 324.4s (depth 4) — a **3.30x increase**.
   - In complex middlegames, diagnostic profiling recorded **19.8 seconds per move** at depth 4 (66,254 nodes evaluated).
   - Pure-Python move generation and minimax recursion exhibit an effective branching factor of $b \approx 3.7$ under alpha-beta pruning.

## 5. Opponent Suitability & Limitations

### Is Unconstrained Stockfish a Meaningful Opponent?
- **As an upper benchmark and reliability stress-test:** Stockfish 19 at fixed depth is an excellent correctness validator. It rigorously exposes illegal move generation bugs, check evasion defects, and board mutation leaks across thousands of moves.
- **As a peer playing-strength calibrator:** **No.** Full-strength Stockfish 19 with NNUE evaluation heavily outclasses Janus's hand-crafted material and piece-square table evaluator. Testing against Stockfish at depth 4 is compute-intensive (~5.4 minutes/game) while providing minimal actionable gradient for fine-grained heuristic tuning.
- **Sample Size Warning:** Four games (Score: 37.5%) represent a tiny pilot to verify runtime and correctness; they **do not prove** that Janus has an Elo of $X$ or that depth 4 is statistically superior to depth 3.

## 6. Recommendations for Next Milestones

1. **Do NOT run 50 or 100 games at Depth 4 immediately:**
   - At 5.4 minutes per game, a 50-game depth-4 match would require $\approx 270\text{ minutes} \approx \mathbf{4.5\text{ hours}}$, and 100 games would take $\mathbf{9\text{ hours}}$.
2. **Profile and Optimize Python Search Throughput First:**
   - Before scaling to deeper matches, profile move generation and evaluation hotspots in `janus.search` and `janus.evaluation` to reduce the ~19s per-move cost at depth 4.
3. **Calibrate Against a Weaker Peer Opponent:**
   - For engine tuning, configure Stockfish with `UCI_LimitStrength = True` and calibrated `UCI_Elo` (or test against a Python-native peer engine) to establish win/loss distributions that reflect realistic playing strength.
