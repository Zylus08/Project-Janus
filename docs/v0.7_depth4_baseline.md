## 1. Executive Summary & Overview

Following the v0.6 search profiling and optimization milestone (which achieved a 5.54x benchmark speedup and a 3.16x match runtime reduction), milestone v0.7 executes the first full 50-game playing-strength baseline at **fixed depth 4** against Stockfish 19.

### Key Results
- **Games Scheduled & Completed:** 50 / 50 (100% completion; 0 crashes, 0 timeouts).
- **Opening Pairs:** 25 pairs sampled from `benchmarks/openings.json` with strict color reversal.
- **Janus Playing Record (W-D-L):** **1 Win, 26 Draws, 23 Losses**.
- **Scoring:** **14.0 / 50.0 points (28.0%)**.
- **Elapsed Match Runtime:** **4,611.0 seconds (76.85 minutes / ~1 hr 17 min)**.
- **Average Game Duration:** **92.2 seconds / game** (median: 71.9s).
- **Average Ply Duration:** **0.857 seconds / ply** across 5,379 total plies.

Compared to the earlier v0.5 50-game baseline at depth 3 (which scored 15.0% and took 4,921.9s), the depth-4 baseline achieved:
1. **Near-doubled scoring rate:** 28.0% at depth 4 vs 15.0% at depth 3 (+13.0 percentage points), primarily driven by defensive resilience and draw conversion (26 draws vs 11 draws).
2. **Superior efficiency:** Depth 4 under v0.6 completed 50 games in **4,611.0s**, running faster than depth 3 pre-optimization (**4,921.9s**) despite searching significantly deeper decision trees.

## 7. Limitations of Fixed-Depth Comparisons

1. **Fixed-Depth Asymmetry:**  
   Fixed depth per move eliminates time controls as a variable, ensuring deterministic test runs. However, fixed depth inherently favors Stockfish: Stockfish searches depth 4 in 2–5 milliseconds per move, whereas Janus requires 0.5–1.5 seconds per move.
2. **Evaluation Heuristics vs NNUE:**  
   Janus relies on a transparent, handcrafted material and PST evaluation function with simple center and king safety bonuses. Stockfish 19 uses an advanced NNUE neural network evaluator trained on millions of positions. At equal search depth (depth 4), Stockfish's static leaf evaluations are vastly more accurate, which accounts for its advantage in non-drawn games.
3. **Statistical Elo Scope:**  
   While 50 games provide a stable baseline for Project Janus, a 50-game sample against a single fixed-depth opponent does not constitute a FIDE/CCRL rating. Rather, it serves as an empirical playing-strength benchmark against which future evaluation, move ordering, and search enhancements will be measured.
