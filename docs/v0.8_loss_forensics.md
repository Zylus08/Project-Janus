## 1. Executive Summary & Forensic Methodology

Following the v0.7 milestone, which established a 50-game playing-strength baseline at fixed depth 4 against Stockfish 19 (scoring 14.0/50, 28.0%), this investigation conducts an empirical forensic analysis of all 23 losses, 26 draws, and the single win.

### Forensic Analysis Protocol
1. **Screening Phase (Depth 10):**
   - For every game ply where Janus was on move, the position was analyzed before and after Janus's move using Stockfish 19 (`threads=1`, `hash=128 MB`, `depth=10`).
   - Evaluation drop was measured:
     $$\Delta_{\text{eval}} = \text{Eval}_{\text{Stockfish}}(\text{Before Janus Move}) - \text{Eval}_{\text{Stockfish}}(\text{After Janus Move})$$
     (normalized strictly to Janus's point of view in centipawns; positive $\Delta$ indicates deterioration).
2. **Deep Verification Phase (Depth 16, MultiPV=3):**
   - Every candidate moment where $\Delta \ge 150\text{ cp}$ or where the position transitioned into a forced mate was re-analyzed at `depth=16` with `MultiPV=3`.
   - The top 3 Stockfish moves, scores, and principal variations (PVs) were recorded alongside Janus's actual move.
3. **Machine-Readable Outputs:**
   - Full dataset saved in [`analysis/v0.8_game_index.json`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.8_game_index.json), [`analysis/v0.8_loss_analysis.json`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.8_loss_analysis.json), [`analysis/v0.8_draw_analysis.json`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.8_draw_analysis.json), and [`analysis/v0.8_win_analysis.json`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/analysis/v0.8_win_analysis.json).

## 3. Analysis of the 23 Losses

Across the 23 losses, the two-stage screening identified **154 critical deterioration moments** where Janus's position suffered an evaluation loss of $\ge 150\text{ cp}$ or transitioned into a forced mate.

### Breakdown of Failure Modes

| Failure Mode | Count | Proportion | Primary Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **King-Safety Failure** | **52** | **33.8%** | King exposed to mating net; search horizon blind spot ($>4$ plies) |
| **Missed Tactical Defence** | **34** | **22.1%** | Failing to respond to multi-move pins, skewers, or deflections |
| **Hanging Material / Failed Gain** | **27** | **17.5%** | Material dropped due to incomplete capture chain or bad exchange |
| **Poor Move Ordering / Horizon** | **19** | **12.3%** | Searching wrong moves first, causing beta cutoff failure or horizon cutoff |
| **Positional Concession** | **18** | **11.7%** | Ceding critical outposts, doubled/isolated pawns, or bishop pair |
| **Endgame Technique** | **4** | **2.6%** | King activity or passed pawn handling in simplified positions ($<10$ pieces) |
| **Total Critical Moments** | **154** | **100.0%** | |

### Key Findings on Failure Modes
1. **The King-Safety Blind Spot (33.8%):**  
   Janus evaluates king safety solely through castling rights (+8/-8 cp in [`src/janus/evaluation.py`](file:///E:/ChessXML/project-janus/src/janus/evaluation.py#L65-L72)). It has **zero dynamic king safety evaluation** (no open file penalties, no pawn shield checks, no attacking zone counts). Consequently, Janus comfortably exposes its king to advance minor pieces, remaining completely oblivious to mating attacks until mate is within its 4-ply search horizon.
2. **Horizon Cutoff in Tactical Sequences (22.1%):**  
   Because Janus searches to fixed depth 4, a 5-ply tactical combination (e.g. `1. Sacrifice 2. King takes 3. Check 4. King moves 5. Fork / Queen loss`) appears completely safe to Janus at depth 4 because the final recapture or fork occurs at depth 5.
3. **Absence of Move Ordering Heuristics (12.3%):**  
   Janus orders moves using static MVV/LVA for captures and arbitrary order for quiet moves. It possesses **no killer moves, no history heuristic, and no check extensions**. In complex non-tactical positions, searching suboptimal quiet moves first wastes tree exploration and prevents deeper tactical pruning.

- **Position (Black to move):** `r4rk1/ppp2ppp/8/8/3q1n2/3P4/PPPK1PPP/R6R b - - 0 28`
- **Context:** Stockfish (White) has an awkwardly placed king on `d2`. Janus as Black has active pieces.
- **Janus Move:** `28...d4e5?`
- **Stockfish Refutation:** Stockfish immediately played `29. Rae1!`, seizing the open file and coordinating with the queen.
- **Stockfish Optimal Move:** `28...Qxf2+!` followed by `29. Kc3 Qxg2`, winning two pawns with a crushing $+6.5$ advantage.
- **Root Cause:** Janus evaluated `Qe5` as centralizing the queen and defending against back-rank tactics, failing to calculate that `Qxf2+` forces the white king into a mating net.

## 5. Analysis of the 26 Draws

Janus drew **26 out of 50 games (52.0%)**, an extraordinary draw rate against Stockfish 19 at fixed depth 4.

### Draw Categories

| Category | Count | Proportion | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Successful Defence from Deficit** | **23** | **88.5%** | Janus trailed by $\ge 2.0\text{ cp}$ during the game, but defended into a threefold repetition |
| **Possible Missed Win Opportunity** | **2** | **7.7%** | Janus reached $\ge +2.0\text{ cp}$ (Games 7 & 17) before allowing perpetual check / repetition |
| **Tactical Balance Maintained** | **1** | **3.8%** | Neither engine achieved a material imbalance ($|\Delta| < 100\text{ cp}$) throughout |

### Why Does Janus Draw So Frequently?
1. **Threefold Repetition Bias:**  
   Janus's search recognizes threefold repetition as score `0`. When under pressure and facing negative evaluation, finding a sequence of checks or repeating piece maneuvers evaluates to `0` (a draw), which alpha-beta aggressively selects over playing a deteriorating position.
2. **Defensive Resourcefulness in Symmetric Pawn Structures:**  
   In classical openings (`scotch_game`, `ruy_lopez_berlin`, `sicilian_dragon`), depth 4 is sufficient to prevent outright single-move tactical blunders. Stockfish at depth 4 cannot see deep positional sacrifices, frequently resulting in mutual repetition loops.

## 6. Anatomy of the Win: Game 32

**Match:** Game 32, Pair 16 (`queens_gambit_declined`), Janus as Black vs Stockfish 19 as White.  
**Result:** `0-1` (Janus wins by checkmate on ply 44 in 50.2 seconds).

### Critical Sequence Analysis

```
1. d4 d5 2. c4 e6 3. Nc3 Nf6 4. Nf3 Nbd7 5. e3 c6 6. Qc2 Bd6 7. b3 O-O 8. Be2 b6
9. O-O Bb7 10. Bb2 c5 11. cxd5 exd5 12. Rfd1 Rc8 13. dxc5 bxc5 14. Nb5? Bb8
```

1. **Ply 13–15 (Stockfish's Opening Imprecision):**
   Stockfish initiated premature central exchanges (`13. dxc5?` and `14. Nb5?`), driving Janus's dark-squared bishop to `b8`, where it lined up menacingly along the `b8-h2` diagonal against White's castled king.
2. **Ply 20–24 (Janus's Dynamic Tactical Exploitation):**
   Janus found sharp, aggressive moves:
   - `20...Bc5!` (pressuring `d4`).
   - `22...Ng4!` (threatening `h2` and `f2`).
   - `24...Bxf2+!` (ripping open White's king shield).
3. **Ply 31–38 (The Decisive Blunder & Checkmate):**
   - On ply 31, Stockfish played `16. e4-e5?`, allowing Black's queen to penetrate via `16...Qh4!`.
   - On ply 35, Stockfish blundered into mate with `18. Nh2-f3??` (evaluating to forced mate in 2).
   - Janus instantly played `18...Qh1+!`, forcing `19. Ng1`, and delivered checkmate with `19...Qxg1#` on ply 38 (ply 44 of the game).

### Strategic Takeaways from Game 32
- Janus's piece-square table and center bonuses rewarded active minor piece piece-play along open diagonals.
- When an opponent blunders into a tactical net within 4 plies, Janus's alpha-beta search refutes it decisively.
- However, Game 32 was enabled by Stockfish 19's shallow 4-ply horizon; this single win must not be construed as general superiority in the Queen's Gambit.

## 7. Forensic Conclusions & Requirements for v0.9

1. **Top Weakness: Complete Lack of Dynamic King Safety (33.8% of losses).**  
   Janus desperately needs an evaluation penalty for exposed kings and missing pawn shields, or search check extensions to look deeper when facing checks.
2. **Move Ordering Deficiencies (12.3% of losses).**  
   Adding **Killer Move Heuristic** and **History Heuristic** will dramatically improve search pruning, allowing Janus to search deeper within the same time budget.
3. **Quiescence Search Tactical Horizon (17.5% of losses).**  
   Quiescence search currently explores all captures equally; adding **Stand-Pat Delta Pruning** will prevent evaluating hopeless capture lines and save nodes for relevant tactical checks.
